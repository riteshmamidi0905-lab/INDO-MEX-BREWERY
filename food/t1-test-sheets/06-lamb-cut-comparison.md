# T1 Comparison: Tandoori Lamb Taco, which lamb cut?

**Status:** TEST DESIGN. **No cut has been tested and none is chosen.** The cut is decided only by the pre-set rule below after real testing. Dish: our own composition (Punjabi tandoori-style marination + Sonoran-style grilling and tortilla logic), **not a historical India-Mexico dish**. Policy: lamb only; no beef or pork; lard-free tortillas. Menu #8 in `food/MENU-V1.1-TEST-MENU.md`.

## 1. Objective and hypothesis
Find the lamb cut (and trim) that best delivers: charred, bright, herb/chile/acid-forward, tender, consistent, workable on a fast grill, and affordable at menu scale.
**H1:** at least one commercially realistic cut meets the pre-set quality floor and yield floor. **H0:** no cut does; then rework the cook method or protein (chicken/goat), do not lower the floor.

## 2. Candidate cuts (to be put to a butcher/supplier; add or remove on their advice)
Boneless leg · shoulder (boneless) · trimmed loin or rack offcuts · any cut the supplier recommends for fast high-heat cooking. Origin (domestic/imported) is a recorded variable, not pre-judged. **Do not pre-select a winner.**

## 3. Controls (identical for every cut)
Same marinade formula and marinating time; same heat source and cooking method; same slicing direction and thickness; same tortilla, salsa and garnish; same plating; coded plates. Cook each cut **at least twice** on different days.

## 8. Pre-set decision rule (write before cooking; defaults below are an ASSUMPTION for the founder/chef to change before use)
1. **Floors:** a cut is eliminated if average tenderness < 3, or "too gamey" flagged by ≥ 2 tasters, or cooked yield below a floor set from the first iteration, or no reliable supplier.
2. Among survivors rank by a weighted score (assumed weights: eating quality 50, consistency 20, cost-to-cooked-yield 20, supplier reliability 10; **change them before cooking if you disagree**). Cost enters only from real quotes.
3. If top two are within the noise (set a margin before cooking), choose the more consistent, then the more reliably supplied.
4. Record the chosen cut, the margin, and what would reverse the decision.

## 9. Differentiation check
Run the chosen cut in a blind pair with the Dum Goat Birria taco: can tasters tell them apart and describe the difference (goat = slow, braised, deep, consommé-driven; lamb = charred, grilled, brighter, faster)? If not, rework.
