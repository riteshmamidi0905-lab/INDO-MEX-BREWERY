**Status:** TEST DOCUMENT. **This dish has not been cooked.** Every blank is to be filled during testing; no value below is a result. Policy: no beef, no pork (D-54), not claimed halal. Menu: `food/MENU-V1.1-TEST-MENU.md` #9. Date: ____  Cook: ____  Iteration #: ____  (min. 3)

## 1. Objective
Prove that a sealed (dum-inspired) low, slow braise of goat in a guajillo-ancho adobo produces deep meat and a drinkable consommé, served as griddled tacos with consommé to dip. It must be clearly different from the Tandoori Lamb Taco.

## 2. Source dishes and traditions (research references, not claims of a shared history)
- Birria (Jalisco goat tradition; consommé and quesabirria forms from Tijuana; `research/MEXICAN-CULINARY-BENCHMARKS.md`)
- Dum braising (sealed-pot technique associated with Hyderabadi cooking; origin stories are folklore, see `research/HISTORICAL-CLAIMS-AUDIT.md`)
- This is our own composition, not a historical India-Mexico dish. **Goat only; no beef.**

## 3. What must remain recognisable (identity checklist)
- Dried-chile adobo (guajillo/ancho/pasilla family) is the spine.
- A long braise produces a drinkable, balanced consommé.
- Tortilla dipped and griddled; onion, cilantro, lime on the side.
- The dum seal and the ghee-curry leaf tadka are noticeable but do not turn it into a curry.

## 4. Variables being tested (change one at a time; record which)
- Goat cut (shoulder vs leg vs mixed) and bone-in vs boneless
- Pre-marinade (yogurt-papaya) time
- Braise time and temperature (record; do not assume)
- Seal method
- Consommé: fat skim level, seasoning, tadka at the table
- Chicken/vegetable stock use (no beef bones)

## 5. Policy and ingredient screen (D-54)
Goat only; stock must be goat/chicken/vegetable; no beef or pork bones, no commercial bases without spec; tortillas lard-free.
