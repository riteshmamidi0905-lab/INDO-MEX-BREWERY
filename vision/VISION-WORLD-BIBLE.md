# NASHA — WORLD BIBLE (VISION LAYER)

*VISION — what NASHA could become. Not evidence, not a plan of record.*

## 1. Emotional north star
**A place where you are looked after, and where you can taste that someone thought about the meal and the glass together.**
Warm, unhurried, generous, a little proud. People should feel the pleasure of arriving at a good table, not the effort of understanding a concept.

## 2. One world, not a mashup
NASHA is not half India, half Mexico. Many food cultures use a courtyard as a room, fire as a main tool, chile and acid as structure and a shared table as the point; we treat that as an analogy to explore, not as a claim of shared history. NASHA takes the **structure**, not the souvenir. Cultural reference appears as proportion, material, pattern logic, craft, food, hospitality and objects with a purpose. Never as costume.

**Banned:** flags, map mashups, sombreros, the Taj Mahal, elephants, cactus overload, marigold overload, Day of the Dead imagery, Bollywood, random mandalas, generic hacienda or haveli cosplay, sacred or ceremonial motifs used as decoration.

## 4. Day, golden hour, night, rain, summer, winter
- **Day:** limestone, natural light, greenery, craft, food. The court is cool and quiet.
- **Golden hour:** low sun rakes across the court; the oak and the perforated screens throw patterns; the patio fills.
- **Night:** brass, amber, the brewery glowing through glass, social energy. Avoid nightclub lighting.
- **Rain:** the arcades become a dry ring around a wet, dark court; the rill rises; the room feels sheltered.
- **Summer:** shade, fans, cold glasses, water, zero-proof coolers lead.
- **Winter:** closed-in warmth, braise and consomme, malt-forward beers, the oven mouth glowing in the north arcade.

## 5. The Court Brewhouse (the architectural heart)
VISION FLAGSHIP — NOT CONSTRUCTION DESIGN. Built on the canonical scene (`visuals/LOCKED-SCENE-DNA.md`): a double-height limestone-and-glass **Tank Hall** on the west; a one-storey restaurant block on the north; **arcades** on three sides; a **shade court** with gravel, a shallow stone rill, cedar elms and communal pecan tables; an open lawn and live oaks on the east.

**The spatial story**
1. **Street.** A low, quiet profile: limestone, a dark roof, glass glowing. The tanks are glimpsed, not advertised.
2. **Threshold.** A covered recess beside the hall corner. A cedar soffit lowers the ceiling. The air is cooler.
3. **Entry compression.** A short lobby along the hall's south glass: a low, dark, warm passage. You smell grill smoke and malt before you see the court.
4. **Court reveal.** The passage opens: sky, shade, oak, a long rill, tables, voices.
5. **Brewhouse.** The hall's east glass faces the court: six tanks in a horseshoe with a thin orange sleeve each, a brew deck behind. The brewery is a neighbour, not a feature.
6. **Bar and dining.** The bar sits where the hall meets the restaurant, its backbar window looking into the tanks. Dining rooms open to the court.
7. **Patio at night.** The court deepens: uplit tanks, glowing oven mouth, warm arcades, a loud-but-human hum.

**The tanks are craft, not decoration.** Stainless, working, accessible by catwalk, drained and cleaned. No fake copper vessels. Copper appears only as a hood detail and small bar objects (V-03).

## 6. Arrival (first thirty seconds)
**Day.** From the street you see a calm limestone-and-glass frontage and a shaded arcade. One partial tank behind glass. A line of cedar-lined shade pulls you in. The sign is a plain steel panel, not shouting. The door is warm bronze and glass. You hear the court before you see it. You smell charred corn and fresh bread. Recognisable: the horseshoe of tanks and the perforated screen.
**Night.** The hall glows amber and the tanks are the brightest large objects in the street. Entry is lit low, so the court is a reveal. The brewery is first seen through the lobby glass at the moment of compression; the court noise arrives a second later.

## 7. Dining room — seven social conditions
| Moment | Where and how |
|---|---|
| **Date** | A corner two-top by the court window, shielded light, two glasses, one shared plate. |
| **Two friends** | Bar corner or a low court seat: food between them, beer and zero-proof side by side. |
| **Four friends** | A round of four-tops beside a communal table; a shared tray and mixed pours. |
| **Family** | Early evening in the dining room, near the court; kids in a clearly separate, non-alcohol rhythm, zero-proof for everyone. |
| **Large table** | The long pecan table in the court: dishes in the middle, servers stay near. |
| **Bar solo** | The pecan bar, with a view into the brewery and a kind bartender. |
| **Beer explorer** | A small flight, a bartender who talks, a seat at the backbar window. |
Inclusive by design: step-free routes, comfortable chairs, a quieter side, a livelier side.

## 8. The bar
A long dark pecan top; blackened-steel structure; a handmade matte tile front in bone with a broken orange stripe; about eight handles (the five core beers, one rotating, two zero-proof) each with one orange ring; open steel shelving with the glass family; the brewery visible beyond. It serves house beer, zero-proof pairings, water and food. It belongs to the food system: the pairing line and the dish names are visible from the stool.

## 9. The brewery (vision scale)
Six tanks in a horseshoe, a small brew deck, a catwalk, trench drains, a clean seamless floor, a cold-side beside the bar. CIP lines and cleaning logic visible as tidy tri-clamp piping, not styled. Guests see it from four places and never step on the production floor. **Professional engineering overrides every dimension.**

## 10. The kitchen
Selective visibility: the pass window to the north arcade, the glowing mouth of the enclosed oven, the heat on the cooks' faces. Fry, char, comal, sauce, prep and pass exist as stations, not as a show. Exhaust stacks are plain blackened steel.

## 11. Sound
Quiet, warm, human. Day: acoustic, instrumental, low tempo. Evening: the tempo rises gently. No stereotyped India-plus-Mexico playlist. Austin influence means room for the local: singer-songwriters, a live set in the alcove is an open question. Licensing is unresolved and nothing is claimed.

## 12. Guests and people
Show varied adults, families with children clearly separated from alcohol, staff and brewers. No identifiable real person. No minors near alcohol. No tokenism: people are doing something, eating, laughing, listening.

## 13. Cultural respect
Vision mode does not waive review. See `data/VISION-CULTURAL-FLAGS.csv`. No claim of authenticity. No sacred or ceremonial motif as decoration. Everything flagged waits for future Indian and Mexican cultural reviewers.
