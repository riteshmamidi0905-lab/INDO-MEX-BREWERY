## 1. The question
**What is the smallest credible operation that can prove people will repeatedly pay for this food and become interested in the larger brand?** Not a miniature restaurant on wheels.

## 2. What the (unverified) rules appear to say about mobile operation
| Topic | Finding (tier) | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Permit authority | Austin's city page says mobile food vending administration is moving to the state (DSHS); from **2026-07-01** vendors need a **state Mobile Food Vending Permit** (T1, V-23; verify current process) | Check DSHS requirements directly before any plan |
| Commissary | Austin guidance requires a central preparation facility (a permitted fixed food business, not a home); DSHS rule text also allows some units to operate without a commissary if checklist requirements are met (T1, V-24, V-25; sources of mixed age) | A commissary or shared kitchen is likely a recurring dependency |
| Food manager and handlers | Certified food protection manager for open TCS food; handler training for others (T1, V-25) | Training cost and a named person |
| Fire | Propane/gas units need a fire safety inspection (T1, V-24) | Equipment choice affects approvals |
| Units | Self-powered truck or towable trailer; permit covers equipment on the vehicle (T1, V-24) | Constrains cooking equipment |
| **Alcohol** | TABC alcohol permits go to a **permanent physical address**; a moving truck has no qualifying address (T1, V-26). A third-party food truck on a licensed premises counts toward the holder's 60% sales test (Option 2) but not as the holder's own food facility (T1, 2022 notice, V-27) | **A food truck cannot sell our beer or alcohol**; beer links must run through licensed partners; attorney needed (`docs/decisions/ATTORNEY-QUESTIONS.md`) |
| Costs | National vendor content (T3, V-31): trailers roughly $25k-$130k all-in depending on size/fit-out; trucks roughly $30k-$200k (used vs new); permits a few $k; commissary rent extra. **Not Texas-specific; not quotes** | Use only to rank options, never as a budget |
| Brewery deals | Anecdotal (T3, V-32): flat monthly fees, a percentage of sales (one operator reported 10%), or no fee with shared costs; breweries want food for traffic | Terms are negotiable and unverified; no standard contract found |

## 4. Which currently looks most useful (provisional, reversible)
**Best learning per dollar today appears to be a combination, in this order:**
1. **Commissary/shared-kitchen pop-ups and tastings** (lowest asset commitment; test dishes, pairings and interest; works for private tastings and dinners; reversible).
2. **Brewery residencies or repeated brewery-hosted pop-ups** (closest to the food-and-beer DNA; repeat visits and real volume; the host handles alcohol; terms and legality to be confirmed).
3. **A food trailer** only *after* pop-ups/residencies show repeat demand and a stable menu; it is cheaper than a truck in vendor content (T3) and more reversible.
4. A food truck is the **least attractive first asset** (highest capital and permit load, no alcohol, constrained kitchen); consider only if a route/visibility advantage is proven.
5. Farmers/event markets are useful for awareness and low-risk sampling, but poor for repeat data and brand control.
**Why this order:** it tests the three riskiest assumptions (will people like it, will they return, can we execute at pace) before buying an asset. **What would change the ranking:** a verified commissary cost, a brewery that actually offers a residency, permit surprises, or interview data showing mobile format interest.
**Unknowns that decide it:** commissary/shared-kitchen availability and cost in Austin; a willing brewery partner; permit and fire path; whether a licensed partner can legally pair or collaborate on beer; cost of hire vs ownership. All `TBD`.

## 5. Proposed MVP menu (from the 12-dish test set; **untested, proposed**)
Target: 4-6 core items + 1-2 snacks + 1 dessert + non-alcoholic drinks. Chosen for identity, throughput, ingredient cross-use, vegetarian share and repeatability. **No dish is validated.**
| Role | Item | What it tests | Shared inputs | Equipment (planning assumption) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core 1 | **Paneer Tikka Taco** (veg) | Veg appeal; "makes sense" fusion; charred-marinade execution | corn tortilla, hari, achari onion | grill/charbroiler or flat-top |
| Core 2 | **Gobi Taco** (veg) | Crisp fried veg at pace; repeat veg order | corn tortilla, slaw, Imli-Guajillo | fryer |
| Core 3 | **Butter Chicken Rajas Quesadilla** | Gateway/family dish; cheese-pull crowd pleaser | flour tortilla, Raita-Crema | flat-top/comal |
| Core 4 | **Goat Birria taco with consommé cup** (limited batch) | Hardest differentiation; goat supply/price tolerance; sell-out frequency | corn tortilla, onion/cilantro/lime | commissary braise; hot-hold |
| Core 5 (conditional) | **Tandoori Lamb Taco** | Lamb differentiation vs birria; premium price tolerance | flour tortilla, hari | grill; **only after the lamb-cut test** |
| Snack 1 | **Chicken 65 Totopos** | The identity/shareable snack; fryer fit | masa chips, Lal sauce | fryer |
| Snack 2 | **Chaat Elote** (veg) | Cheap, fast, vegetarian "wow" | corn, crema-raita | grill |
| Dessert | **Cardamom Churros** | Margin and shareable finish | fryer | fryer |
| Drinks (NA) | House **aguas** (hibiscus, tamarind; proposed) | Beverage attach; pairing story without alcohol | n/a | cold dispensers |
**Held back for the MVP:** Dal-Frijol Dip, Hefe-Brined Wings (needs our beer; consider as a partner-brewery collaboration special, legality TBD), Malabar Fish Taco (fish handling/cost), Mole-Salan Bowl (peanut/sesame allergens; later), all Wave 2 and conditional dishes.
**Vegetarian representation (as designed):** 2 of 5 core + elote + churros (strict status pending rennet/stock verification). **Cross-use:** two tortilla types, five shared sauces/garnishes. **Hard dependencies:** lard-free tortillas, no beef/pork stock, goat and lamb suppliers (all unconfirmed).

## 7. Mobile brand experience (how the whole brand shows up small)
**Message: "This is the beginning of something bigger", without implying a brewery exists.**
| Element | Proposed approach |
|---|---|
| Exterior / stall | Calm, architectural, not costume: blackened steel and bone, one narrow marigold line, the ring/collar mark as the recurring motif; no clichés |
| Menu board | One page, heat scale, "our own dish / inspired by" lines, no beef/pork line only after kitchen verification, no halal claim |
| Packaging | Black-stoneware-toned stock, compostable if viable, ring mark, QR |
| Uniforms | Charcoal canvas aprons, bone shirts, marigold stitch (brand system) |
| Music / sound | Restrained; no cultural-stereotype playlists |
| Typography | One grotesque + restrained serif (no faux-script) |
| Photography | Real food, real hands, real events, honestly labelled; no staged crowds |
| QR experience | Menu, pairing notes, "follow the build", signup (consent-based); links to website Journal/Lab |
| Pairing education | "What to drink with this" cards naming **partner brewery beers** where available; no alcohol sales from the unit; advertising rules need attorney review |
| Merchandise | Small, honest: stickers/aprons/printed ring mark before glassware |
| Founder story | Plain, real, ongoing; **no invented origin story (D-09)** |
| Future-brewery teaser | "A brewery is the plan; today we are testing the food" style line; **never "brewed by us" while we do not brew** |
| Name | Working label until trademark work completes; founder-proposed NASHA family under review (`brand/BRAND-ARCHITECTURE-V1.md` §4b) |

## 8. What not to build
No full kitchen on wheels; no custom truck wrap or expensive fit-out before repeat evidence; no inventory of merchandise; no paid advertising until economics justify it; no alcohol sales plan on a mobile unit.

## 9. Sequence (dependency-based, no dates)
Interviews and private tastings → T1 food tests → permit/commissary check (state/Austin) → first pop-ups/tastings with partners (attorney confirms beer/pairing arrangements) → measurement design live from event 1 → brewery residency conversations → repeat-demand decision → trailer decision. Each step has an exit criterion in the traction doc.
