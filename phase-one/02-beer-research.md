# 6-8. Beer Research

## 6. Indian beer
**[VERIFIED S-11, S-12]** Bira 91 launched in 2015 from B9 Beverages, founded by Ankur Jain. Early production was contract-brewed in Belgium before moving to India. Press at the time reported rapid growth and a position against mass-market incumbents; growth figures vary by source and are 2017-18 vintage. Treat as history, not current performance.

**[UNCITED]** Indian beer is dominated by strong lagers (Kingfisher/UB, a high-ABV "strong" category is large in volume). Craft beer is concentrated in metros (Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Pune, Hyderabad), with wheat beers, witbier-style and pale ales popular because they pair with spiced food. Brewpubs such as Toit (Bengaluru) established the craft-brewpub model. Brand-level claims for Simba, White Owl, BeeYoung, Maka Di, Geist, Arbor need verification: **do not cite them until checked**. Recent reports of financial trouble at some craft brands must be checked before using any as a "success story."

**Lessons to take (concept, not copy):** wheat and witbier lead craft in a spiced-food culture; the beer must work with heat; local ingredients (mango, ginger, coriander, pepper) are already used; serving in brewpubs with big menus works. **Do not copy** names, labels, packaging or recipes.

## 7. Mexican beer
**[VERIFIED S-08, S-09, S-10]** German and Austrian brewers shaped Mexican brewing, with the Vienna lager style taking root during the 1860s era around Maximilian (sources differ on how directly). Cervecería Cuauhtémoc (Monterrey, ~1890-91, Carta Blanca), Cervecería Moctezuma (Orizaba, Veracruz; Siglo XX which became Dos Equis), Modelo (Mexico City, 1922) and Bohemia (1905, Pilsner-style) are the key houses. Consolidation into two giants followed; Cuauhtémoc Moctezuma is now Heineken-owned (per S-10; verify). Dates vary by source; corroborate before publishing.

**[UNCITED]** Modern Mexican craft beer (Baja, Mexico City, Guadalajara) uses local ingredients: chile, hibiscus (jamaica), agave, cacao, coffee, tropical fruit. Mexican lager is typically light, crisp and served very cold with lime and salt in warm weather.

**Lesson:** The Vienna-lager heritage gives a *credible* Mexican beer style that isn't just "pale lager with lime." That is where our amber/dark classics belong.

## 8. German/Belgian wheat beer (founder's priority)
**[VERIFIED S-06]** BJCP 2021 style 10A (Weissbier): OG 1.044-1.057, FG 1.008-1.014, IBU 10-18, SRM 14-23, ABV 4.3-5.6%. High carbonation, dry finish, fluffy mouthfeel; banana and clove from the yeast; bubblegum is a fault. 10B is Dunkles Weissbier.
**[VERIFIED S-07]** The "at least 50% malted wheat" rule is from the Brewers Association style guidelines, not BJCP.

**Technique notes (all `[UNCITED]`, verify with brewing texts and a licensed brewer; `[TEST]` in pilot):**
- Banana (isoamyl acetate) is promoted by moderate-to-warmer fermentation, lower pitch rate and less pressure; clove (4-vinyl guaiacol) by ferulic acid rest around 43-45 °C (110-113 °F). Different recipes balance them. Open fermentation is traditional in some breweries.
- Carbonation is high (roughly 3.0-4.0 volumes CO₂ `[TEST]`) and drives the foam.
- Vase-shaped weizen glass: tall, narrow base, wide top for foam. Rinse glass with cold water, pour at an angle, then swirl the yeast from the last inch (hefe) into the final pour.
- Witbier: unmalted wheat, oats, coriander seed and orange peel; Belgian yeast. Dunkelweizen: darker melanoidin character with the same yeast. Weizenbock: stronger (~6.5-8%).
- Pairing: wheat beer's carbonation, fruity esters and low bitterness cut through chile heat and fat. This is a key reason to make Hefeweizen the flagship.

**Gate:** No regional/experimental beer launches until the Hefeweizen and Mexican lager are consistently excellent, validated by a licensed head brewer and independent tasting.
