# Family Destination Expansion: Adventure Court, Game Hall, Flagship Outlet

Status: **concept, approved for study only.** Recommended zoning: **Option C+** (`FAMILY-ZONING-OPTIONS.md`). The brewery stays the heart; none of these may compromise the Tank Cloister, kitchen, service routes or the Shade Court's conversation. All safety, code and cost items are `[PRO]`. Footprints are order-of-magnitude estimates `[C]`, not measurements.

## 1. Adventure Court (outdoor)
**Defining idea: landscape architecture + play, built from the same materials as the brewery.** Not a playground bolted onto a patio.
- **Where:** the east lawn beyond the Shade Court, bounded on the north by the restaurant block / Game Hall extension and on the south by a low planted edge (not the parking). Live oaks already shade the east; kids never beside the bar, parking, service or brewery operations.
- **Components (natural, tactile):** timber climbing structures (cedar/ dark pecan-stained), rope nets and rope bridges, climbing walls set into earth mounds, tunnels through small hills, balance and stepping elements, slides built into the landscape slope, scrambling boulders (limestone), sensory planting and sand/water-free sensory boards, a grain-touch drawer wall at the edge (see Interactive History). No primary-colour plastic towers, cartoon figures, castles, pirate ships or mascots.
- **Two zones:** a **younger-child zone** and an **older-child zone**, separated by planting and low rails. Conventional age groups in public-playground guidance are roughly 2-5 and 5-12 `[V S-67 via summaries; confirm age groups in the CPSC Handbook and ASTM F1487]`. **Do not finalize ages until a playground-safety inspector (CPSI) reviews** `[PRO]`.
- **Surfacing:** ASTM F1292 impact-attenuating surfacing under all fall zones; the CPSC handbook uses fall height and a **6-ft minimum fall zone** around equipment (as summarized by Massachusetts guidance); engineered wood fiber or other tested loose-fill plus a **bonded, accessible route** `[PRO]`. Do not rely on any single table; check Table 2 of the Handbook `[V S-67 limited]`.
- **Accessible play:** ADA 2010 Standards §240/§1008: accessible routes, accessible ground-level components of each type and a share of elevated components; sources disagree on slope figures (1:16 vs 1:20), so verify §1008 directly `[V S-69; PRO]`.

### Austin heat (research-based)
- **Surface heat is the main risk.** Measurements show dark or black wet-pour rubber and synthetic turf are the hottest playground surfaces (maxima above 80 °C in Sydney); shade brings surfaces below burn thresholds; a US study of 103 playgrounds found two-thirds had no shade at 10 am to 2 pm and natural materials stayed cooler `[V S-68a,b; the US study's year/URL needs verification]`. News measurements report rubber surfacing at 160-170 °F in sun and about 90 °F in shade `[S-68c, C-grade]`.
- **Design response:** (1) **architectural shade**: a board-formed-concrete-and-cedar-elm frame with a standing-seam roof (the arcade language) covering about **60%** of the play area, plus mature oaks; no cheap fabric sails as the primary solution; (2) **light-coloured, natural, low-conductivity surfaces** (engineered wood fiber, limestone, grass); (3) **no dark rubber**; (4) fans under shade; (5) **drinking-water fountains and bottle fillers**; (6) night and winter use; (7) summer hours shifted to morning and evening; (8) misting only if validated `[PRO]`.
- **Orientation:** open to the south-east breeze `[Gap: confirm wind]`, shade falling from the west on afternoons.

### Parents
- **Sightlines:** a raised limestone **perimeter terrace** with family tables, benches and shaded landscape seating, between the Shade Court and the play zones; every play element visible from at least two seating points; the Game Hall's glazed face also looks onto the play area.
- **Family service point:** a small pass window on the terrace (water, house sodas, fruit drinks, snacks, kids' meals, desserts, a future kulfi/paleta concept, soft serve). Same materials as the building; not a carnival stand.
- **Control:** one gated entry, low blackened-steel fencing with latch, staff sightline, no unsupervised access to service yard, parking, brewery or kitchen `[PRO]`.

## 2. Game Hall (indoor)
**Defining idea: a curated, acoustically isolated room, not an arcade chain.** 15-30 carefully chosen games.
- **Mix to research:** pinball, skee-ball, air hockey, foosball, shuffleboard, basketball hoops, a few driving games, classic cabinets and cooperative modern games. Local precedent: Cidercade (flat price, 150+ games) and Pinballz (free entry, pay per game) `[V S-70a,b]`. Pricing model choice (free / pay-per-play / card / hybrid) is a business question (see operations).
- **Architecture:** dark acoustic ceiling, blackened steel, timber, bone surfaces and a restrained marigold accent; the machines supply colour. No neon signage, no casino lighting, no ticket-redemption clutter.
- **Acoustics:** double-wall and floated-structure options, an **acoustic vestibule** with two sets of doors, absorptive ceiling and walls, acoustic glazing to the Adventure Court; the stage's thick back wall sits between the Game Hall and the court `[PRO acoustician]`.
- **Safety and visibility:** large glazed face to the Adventure Court; one controlled entrance; no hidden back rooms; electrical and egress per code `[PRO]`.

## 3. Flagship Outlet ("cultural design store + brewery shop")
- **Where:** the first two bays of the south arcade beside the entrance recess, with **its own exterior door** and an interior door to the lobby, so guests can shop without a meal; its own opening hours possible.
- **Products (all subject to licensing/production):** cans and mixed packs, growlers/crowlers, ring-collar glassware, flight sets, apparel, aprons, posters, prints, coasters, openers, glass carriers, house sauces, spice blends, coffee and non-alcoholic collaborations, gift boxes, chef/artist collaborations, books and cookbooks, regional food maps.
- **Texas to-go beer:** reporting says brewpubs already could sell some beer for off-premise consumption (growlers filled on site), HB 908 (2017) allowed crowlers and growlers of draft beer, and HB 1545 (2019) let production breweries sell one 24-pack or nine 32-oz crowlers per customer per day `[V S-72, B-grade, 2017-2019]`. **Current rules for a brewpub, packaging, can sales and the retail wall are unresolved** `[PRO: attorney/TABC]`.
- **The Glass Wall:** a minimal, carefully lit wall of ring-collar glasses in a blackened-steel grid; repetition, reflection, shadow, nothing else. Emotion: *I want that glass.*
- **Beer wall (if legal):** blackened-steel refrigerated doors with generic can families; no final can branding.
- **Back-of-house:** receiving through the service yard, not the front door.

## 5. Protecting the brewery
Grain delivery, brewhouse, tank service, kegging, cold storage, spent-grain removal, food delivery, waste, mechanicals and staff circulation stay on the north service side. Children never reach the kitchen, loading dock, yard, parking traffic, alcohol storage or mechanical areas.
