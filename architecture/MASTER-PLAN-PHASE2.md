## 1. The composition in one paragraph
A flat-roofed, double-height **Tank Hall** (44×60 ft, 38 ft) with a limestone plinth, bronze-mullioned glass on the south and east and a 10-ft board-formed concrete canopy anchors the west. A one-storey **restaurant block** (140 ft, extendable) runs east along the north of the **Shade Court** (~110×70 ft with 14-ft arcades on west, north and south). The **entrance recess** sits beside the hall's southeast corner; the **Flagship Outlet** takes the first south-arcade bays with its own door. East of the court, an **east lawn with live oaks** carries the **Adventure Court**; the **Game Hall** terminates the restaurant block at its east end behind an acoustic vestibule (Option C+). A hidden **service yard** on the north holds loading, grain, kegs, spent-grain, mechanical and wastewater equipment.

## 5. Scale reconciliation: visual canon vs. real brewing capacity (new finding)
The locked tank geometry (5 ft diameter, 12-ft shell, 4-ft cone) is **about 63 bbl gross (~50 bbl working)** per fermenter by cylinder-plus-cone arithmetic (1 US bbl = 31 gal = 4.14 ft³). Six of them would be a ~300 bbl fill (6 × ~50 working). The copper vessels shown (~7 ft diameter) are also ~60 bbl-class. That is a **production-brewery scale**, not a brewpub scale, and it conflicts with the evidence on demand:
| Fermenter nominal size (gross) | Dia × height if scaled from the canon | Working vol. each | Six tanks ideal bbl/yr (12 / 20 / 26 turns) | At ~65% utilisation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **~15 bbl** | ~3.1 × 11.8 ft | ~12 bbl | 860 / 1,440 / 1,870 | ~560-1,220 |
| **~30 bbl** | ~3.9 × 14.8 ft | ~24 bbl | 1,730 / 2,880 / 3,740 | ~1,120-2,430 |
| **~60 bbl (visual canon)** | ~4.9 × 18.7 ft | ~48 bbl | 3,460 / 5,760 / 7,490 | ~2,250-4,870 |
Turns assume ~30-, ~18- and ~14-day tank cycles (lager-heavy to ale-heavy). **Demand check:** the Brewers Association benchmark of a **median ~2.6 bbl of on-site beer per seat per year** (taproom/brewpub, 2023 data, `S-76`) gives ~780 bbl for 300 seats (the Recommended scenario seats ~330), in the range of the 15-bbl scenario, **not** the canon. Texas brewpubs also face a 10,000-bbl cap per location (tier T1, `research/VERIFICATION-LEDGER.md` V-01; attorney to confirm), far above any of these.
**Decision (reversible, D-51):** the **visual canon stays** for architecture and communication (it is what ARC-01-D shows), but **real vessel sizing is a brewer's decision driven by demand**, and the feasibility model uses the **~15-30 bbl** scenarios. If real tanks are 12-15 ft tall instead of 19 ft, the 38-ft hall becomes visually over-scaled for the equipment; options for the architect: lower the hall, use the mezzanine and catwalk to give the hall purpose, add a barrel room later, or accept generous volume as the architecture. `[PRO: architect and brewing consultant]`

## 7. What the architect/engineers must resolve (do not pre-judge)
1. Real vessel sizes and their effect on hall height (section 5).
2. Whether the Game Hall fits as an eastward extension or a pavilion.
3. Court thermal strategy (shade, air movement, water) with a mechanical/environmental engineer; the 5-10 °F target is a design hypothesis.
4. Flood, tree and impervious-cover constraints on the preferred site.
5. Hot-side vent, kitchen exhaust and grain-dust placement.
6. Noise: stage orientation and neighbour limits (`[PRO]`).
7. Phase-A provisions for Phase B and C.
