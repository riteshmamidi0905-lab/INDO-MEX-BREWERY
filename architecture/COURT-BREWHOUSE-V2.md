# The Court Brewhouse v2 (stress-tested; NOT final)

Supersedes the Phase One architecture sketch (kept). **Conceptual layouts are not engineering drawings.** All structural, MEP, code, fire, flood, ADA and zoning matters `[PRO]`.

## 1. Thesis, restated
A courtyard house organizes life around an open center. We organize the brewery around **the tanks**. The icon is **the Tank Cloister**: a horseshoe of fermenters standing like an arcade of columns, with the brewhouse on a raised deck at its closed end, all inside a double-height glass hall, opening on the other side to a shaded court. Guests see the beer being made the moment they pass the threshold.

## 2. Configuration options tested
Scores 1-5 (5 best) `[C]`; Cost and Heat are "higher = better (cheaper/easier)".
| Option | Visibility | Ops efficiency (piping, CIP, access) | Cost | Safety separation | Heat/humidity control | ADA | Distinctiveness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Tanks behind one glass wall (typical) | 3 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 1 | Everyone does it |
| B. **Horseshoe "Tank Cloister" in a glazed hall** | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 5 | Brewhouse at the closed end; guests walk the open end/viewing gallery |
| C. Circular tank ring | 5 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 5 | Piping/CIP and access awkward; highest drama |
| D. Elevated tank deck over bar | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 | Load, spill, and guest-safety issues |
| E. Sunken brewhouse well | 4 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 1 | 4 | Flood risk in Austin, ADA, drainage |
| F. Linear fermentation gallery with mezzanine catwalk | 4 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | Reasonable; less iconic |
**Pick: B**, with F's mezzanine gallery as a build-phase simplification, and C/D/E rejected for now.

## 3. The Tank Hall (concept)
- **Hall:** ~40-50 ft clear height at center `[Unresolved: vessel heights drive it]`; clerestory glazing north-facing to avoid solar gain.
- **Tanks:** polished stainless shells; insulated cones clad matte charcoal; a single marigold paint band at mid-shell; large white stencil numbers; neat sanitary piping with consistent routing; manways facing guests. Count/size set by the brewer for target barrelage `[PRO: brewer]`.
- **Brewhouse:** copper-clad kettle and mash tun (copper is a legitimate brewing tradition and was used in the Brewpoint design `[V S-39a]`) on a raised deck at the head of the horseshoe, visible from the entrance; hot-side steam captured by hoods and vented upward.
- **Viewing:** a ground-level glass wall plus a **mezzanine gallery** (ADA-accessible by elevator `[PRO]`) from which guests can look down into the cloister and see brewers working. Brewers' working aisle sits **behind glass**; guests never share the floor with wet, hot, caustic, or CO₂ hazards.
- **Light:** warm 2200-2400K, low-level; tanks softly uplit; nothing flashes.
- **Honesty:** brewing events (mash-in, knock-out, dry-hopping) happen on a posted schedule so the room is *actually* alive.

## 4. The Shade Court (outdoor experience)
Designed as a climate device, not a patio with string lights.
- **Sun:** deep arcade (≈12-15 ft overhang) on south and west edges; perforated steel/concrete screens on the west; light-colored pavers; canopy trees (live oak, cedar elm `[U]`).
- **Air:** high-volume low-speed fans; open ends toward prevailing summer wind `[Gap: believed south-southeast; verify with NOAA]`; the tank hall's glass doors open to let air flow through.
- **Water:** a shallow rill and a splash wall to add evaporative cooling and masking sound; misting only at edge zones, because Austin humidity limits evaporative cooling `[U]`; consult a mechanical/environmental engineer `[PRO]`.
- **Heat reality:** Austin recorded 69 days ≥100°F in summer 2023 and a 45-day streak; the 30-year average is ~33 days/yr `[V S-40a,b]`. Design target: 5-10 °F perceived reduction in peak afternoon zones `[TEST/PRO]`; offer indoor alternatives.
- **Rain:** continuous covered edge so thunderstorms don't empty the room; drainage and flood-plain review `[PRO]`; **thunderstorm mode** (roll-down clear panels) `[PRO]`.
- **Winter:** fire tables (code review), radiant heaters, sun-side seating, glass screens against north winds.
- **Seasonal flexibility:** retractable canvas panels, operable louvers.

## 5. Stress-test questions
| Question | Answer / risk |
|---|---|
| Operationally practical? | Yes if brewhouse and fermenters stay inside a conventional production envelope; horseshoe adds piping length and cost. Needs brewer sign-off. |
| Can guests actually see brewing? | Yes via entrance sightline, ground glass, and mezzanine. Real events must be scheduled. |
| Texas heat? | See §4. Indoor fallback required. |
| Thunderstorms? | Covered edges + drainage + flood review + closable panels. |
| Winter? | Heaters/fire tables; glass screens. |
| How does the brewery smell? | Wort boil smells sweet/malty and can be strong `[U]`; spent grain handling can smell. Place kettle vent and grain out-door **downwind of the court**; keep kitchen exhaust separate `[PRO]`. |
| Sound? | Concrete + glass echo: needs acoustic ceilings/panels; stage faces away from neighbors; City of Austin sound ordinance `[PRO]`. |
| Where does live music go? | A stage alcove at the court's far end with its back to the arcade; indoor acoustic corner near the bar. |
| Children/families? | Daytime family hours; a shaded lawn zone with games, away from stage and hot-side hazards; allergen labeling; no access to brew floor. |
| ADA? | Accessible routes to all public zones, ramps/elevator to the mezzanine, accessible restrooms/bar height sections `[PRO]`. |
| Kitchen to table? | Kitchen sits **between** tank hall and court so food reaches both; runner lanes separate from guest paths. |
| Delivery trucks? | Rear service court with separate road access: grain (bulk bags or silo), kegs, food. No truck crosses guest paths. |
| Grain and kegs? | Mill room near the brewhouse; keg cooler adjacent to the loading dock and bar. |
| Wastewater? | Brewery effluent has high organic load and variable pH; trade-waste pretreatment, floor drains with slope, trap sizing `[PRO]`; check Austin Water rules. |
| Safe separation? | Glass barrier, guarded mezzanine, CO₂ detectors, chemical room away from public, hot-work permits, PPE zones `[PRO]`. |

## 6. Build-phasing option (cost control)
Phase 1: tank hall + bar + kitchen + covered court. Phase 2: arcade extension, second row of tanks, barrel room. Never build a space whose only purpose is a photograph.

## 7. Risks
Capex is above a normal taproom `[Unresolved]`; Austin brewery market is consolidating `[V S-27a]`; complexity of visible brewing raises brewer-workflow compromises; building too big for realistic revenue. Mitigation: partner brewer review, phased build, pop-ups to validate demand.
