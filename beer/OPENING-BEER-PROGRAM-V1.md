# Opening Beer Program v1 (Phase 2)

> HISTORICAL for the gate rule: see beer/pilot-briefs/00-BREWER-HANDOFF.md section 0b and D-77 (proposed replacement; founder confirmation pending).

**Nothing here has been brewed.** Every number is an **R&D target `[TEST]`**, not a validated recipe. Reference ranges are BJCP 2021 (verified through search extracts of the official pages, `S-107..S-108`, medium-high confidence; open the PDF before external use) or flagged otherwise. No commercial-scale recipe, grist percentage, hop weight or ingredient dose is invented: where a dose exists it is a homebrew/supplier reference, labelled as such. A licensed head brewer `[PRO]` owns the final specifications. Builds on `beer/PORTFOLIO-V2.md`, `HEFEWEIZEN-BENCHMARK.md`, `GLASSWARE-V2.md`, `food/MENU-DEVELOPMENT-MATRIX-V1.md`.

## 1. Program principle
1. **Credibility first:** the Classics pass sensory benchmarks before any Regional beer launches (Gate rule, `PORTFOLIO-V2.md`).
2. **Ingredient-job rule:** every non-standard ingredient names its job (aroma, acidity, bitterness, mouthfeel, finish, pairing). "Because it's Indian/Mexican" is rejected.
3. **Distinct without gimmicks:** distinctness comes from (a) classical beers done unusually well, (b) a few regional beers where an ingredient does a real job, and (c) a pairing system with the food. No novelty-for-novelty beers, no chile heat as a standard ingredient.
4. **Honest scale:** six fermenters, one brewhouse; lagers occupy tanks longer than ales; the tap list is a **tank-time budget** (section 6).

### 3.7 Dunkelweizen / Malabar Dark (winter)
BJCP 10B: OG 1.044-1.057, FG 1.008-1.014, IBU 10-18, SRM 14-23, ABV 4.3-5.6. Target ABV 5.0-5.4, IBU 12-16, SRM 15-20. Serve 6-8 °C, Collar Weizen. Food: Dum Goat Birria, Mole-Salan Bowl, biryani. **Malabar Dark pilot:** Dunkelweizen + **black pepper** (job: peppery aroma/finish; homebrew reference ~0.3-0.7 g/L) + **cacao nibs** (job: roast/chocolate complement; homebrew reference ~4 oz per 5 gal ≈ 6 g/L); both unvalidated at scale. Risks: pepper bitterness, cacao fat on foam, TTB status.

### 3.8 Goa Baja (summer regional, **gated**)
Coastal pale lager, ABV 4.3-4.8, IBU 12-18 `[TEST]`. Jobs: **kokum** (sour fruit) = gentle acidity; **sea salt** = mineral/finish; **lime zest** = aroma (not juice). **No usable dose source found for kokum or tamarind**; pilot must establish pH/dose. Salt reference: gose practice ~0.5-0.9 g/L (homebrew). Risks: kokum sourcing and consistency, acid stability, TTB. **Launch only if pilot proves it beats the Mexican Lager's refreshment on a hot-day blind test.** Serve 2-4 °C, Collar Lager. Food: Malabar Fish Taco, ceviche-style specials.

## 4. Pairing system (beer × food)
Pairing logic `[C]` (cited background: carbonation and fat, `HEFEWEIZEN-BENCHMARK.md` §6; peer-reviewed evidence for alcohol/carbonation/bitterness against chile heat was **not found**; the one academic source found tested milk, not beer):
| Principle | Use |
|---|---|
| Carbonation and clean finish scrub fat | Hefeweizen, Hawa, Pilsner with fried dishes (65, wings, gobi) |
| Low bitterness avoids amplifying chile heat | Keep IBUs low on beers paired to heat-3 dishes (Lal, Macha-Tadka) |
| Malt toast meets char/smoke | Vienna Amber with birria, tandoori, dal |
| Roast/sweet partners rich sauces | Dunkelweizen/Malabar Dark with mole-salan, biryani |
| Spice-aroma echoes | Hefe clove with cardamom/clove; coriander with Hari sauce |
Flights: **First Light** (Hefe, Hawa, Witbier, Pilsner), **The Crossing** (Hefe, Deccan Sol, Goa Baja, a pilot regional), **Rain & Spice** (Witbier, a fruit pilot, Malabar Dark), **Smoke & Amber** (Vienna, Dunkelweizen, Malabar Dark, Weizenbock when available), **Brewer's Table** (rotating five). 4-oz Collar Tulip.

## 6. Tank-time budget (derived; shows why the lineup is short)
Assumes the **~15 bbl fermenter scenario** (~12 bbl working each; `architecture/MASTER-PLAN-PHASE2.md` §5), six fermenters, ~780 bbl/yr (BA median 2.6 bbl/seat × 300 seats, `S-76`), and the volume mix below (assumed). Cycle days are assumptions: ale ~14, wheat lager ~21, lager ~30, seasonals ~18.
| Beer | Share (assumed) | bbl/yr | Fills | Days per fill | Tank-days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hefeweizen | 30% | 234 | 19.5 | 14 | 273 |
| Mexican Lager | 20% | 156 | 13.0 | 30 | 390 |
| Vienna Amber | 10% | 78 | 6.5 | 30 | 195 |
| Pilsner | 10% | 78 | 6.5 | 30 | 195 |
| Deccan Sol | 10% | 78 | 6.5 | 21 | 136 |
| Seasonal/regional (4 rotating) | 20% | 156 | 13.0 | 18 | 234 |
| **Total** | | **780** | **65** | | **1,424** (≈237 per tank, **≈65% of 365**) |
Implication: at this scale six fermenters can carry about **8 beers**, and lager volume is what fills tanks. With the canon-scale (~50 bbl) tanks the same demand would leave the tanks mostly empty; this is why real vessel size is a demand-driven decision (D-51). A brite/serving tank and a cold-conditioning plan are separate equipment questions `[PRO]`.
