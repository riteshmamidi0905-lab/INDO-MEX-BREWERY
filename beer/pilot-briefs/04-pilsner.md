# Pilot Brief: Pilsner (working name not yet chosen)

**Status:** brewer-facing brief, written Sprint 16. **Nothing has been brewed.** This is a specification of intent and a test plan, NOT a recipe: no grist, hop weights, water chemistry or pitch rates are given. A qualified brewer owns every production decision (G-6 stays open). Style numbers are BJCP 5D style-typical (T1) or labelled `[TEST]`/T4.

## 1. Intended sensory identity and why NASHA needs it
A brilliant gold, dry, snappy German-style Pilsner with bready pale malt and floral/herbal noble-hop aroma, built as the brewer-credibility beer and the fried/spicy-food reset. It must be **demonstrably different from the Mexican-Style Lager (HAWA)**, which is softer, lower in bitterness, has little hop aroma and may carry a cracker note. Dimensions held apart: hop aroma (Pilsner moderate noble; HAWA at most faint), bitterness (about 28-34 IBU window vs a HAWA window of 14-18 that is UNRECONCILED with BJCP 2A 18-25 and BA 15-22; if HAWA is raised the contrast narrows), dryness/snap (Pilsner drier, snappier), malt (all-malt bready vs cracker/optional adjunct), pairing role (Pilsner: fried food and rich dishes via bitterness; HAWA: chile-heat relief via low bitterness).
**Kill test:** a blind triangle test (panel size and pass criterion stated BEFORE tasting, for example a pre-set number of correct identifications) in which the panel cannot tell Pilsner from HAWA means rework the specs, not delete either beer; a founder decision is required to remove either.

## 3. Faults to exclude
Diacetyl, DMS, sulfur beyond a faint lager note, acetaldehyde, oxidation (cardboard), light-strike, harsh bitterness from high-alkalinity water, haze (clarity is part of the identity).

## 4. Brewer questions
Mash pH against soft, mildly alkaline Austin water (repo water figures are T3 and need a lab test); noble-hop selection, availability and cost; decoction vs infusion; lager time and tank occupancy (about 30 days per lager fill in the tank-time budget); filtration/fining for brilliance; whether a split of a pale-lager batch could trial a Pilsner variant early (the brewer's call).

## 5. Service and pairing hypotheses (NOT SENSORY TESTED)
3-5 C `[TEST]` with a warmer arm (traditional service is warmer and cold mutes noble-hop aroma); slim Pilsner-family glass (concept in `visuals/glass-studies/core5/`, MANUFACTURABILITY UNVERIFIED). Heuristic and expert reasoning favour it with fried and rich food; paneer tikka taco is the first tasting candidate; Chicken 65 Totopos and Mango Habanero Gobi are a deliberate heat-contrast test (bitterness plus chile heat is expected to amplify burn) (`food/CORE5-PAIRING-MATRIX.csv`).
