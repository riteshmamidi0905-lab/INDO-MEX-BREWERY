# Pilot Brief: Mexican-Style Lager (HAWA)

**Status:** brewer-facing brief. **Nothing has been brewed.** This is a *specification of intent and a test plan*, not a recipe. **No** grist, hop weights, spice doses, brewhouse efficiency, water chemistry, pitch rates, fermentation schedules or production yields appear here, and none may be inferred from it: they are the **licensed head brewer's professional responsibility** and come only from real brewing work. ## 1. Intended sensory identity
A clean, crisp, light-bodied pale lager built for hot weather and chile-forward food: cracker/light-grain character, faint sweetness, no skunk, no spice, lime-neutral. We make a well-made Mexican-*style* lager; we do not copy a brand. An adjunct (corn/grits) is allowed only if it improves crispness or aroma, and the brewer must say why.

## 2. Style reference and target ranges
| Item | BJCP reference | Our R&D window `[TEST]` |
|---|---|---|
| OG / FG | International Pale Lager 2A: 1.042-1.050 / 1.008-1.012 (BJCP has no separate Mexican style; a BA Mexican-Style Pale Lager guideline, via a mirror, lists OG 1.045-1.050) | OG 1.044-1.048 |
| ABV | 4.5-6.0% (2A); BA ~4.5-5.0% | 4.4-4.8% |
| IBU | 18-25 (2A); BA ~15-22 | 14-18 |
| SRM | 2-6 | 2-4 |
| Sensory (0-5) | clean, crisp | cracker 2, sweetness 1, bitterness 1, hop aroma ≤1, sulfur/diacetyl/DMS 0 |

## 3. Unacceptable faults (any detectable at panel = batch review)
- Diacetyl, DMS, sulfur beyond a faint lager note, acetaldehyde
- Skunky/light-struck character
- Cloying sweetness or thin watery body
- Visible haze unless intended; any off-flavour a lager exposes
- Water-driven harshness (Austin tap water is soft and mildly alkaline: check)

## 6. Sensory panel structure
- **Panel:** at least 5 trained tasters (brewer, chef, two guests with beer training, one outsider), blind, randomised order, water and plain cracker between samples.
- **Samples:** pilot beer plus 2-3 commercial reference beers (below) plus one duplicate for consistency.
- **Scoring:** 0-5 per attribute (cracker/grain, sweetness, bitterness, hop aroma, fermentation off-notes, clarity, carbonation, finish), plus overall quality and "would order a second" yes/no.
- **Records:** `beer/pilot-log/` (create when brewing begins): batch ID, date, observations, scores, decisions.

## 7. Reference beers (choose and record at P2; availability and exact specs to be confirmed)
- A widely available Mexican pale lager and a Texas-brewed Mexican-style lager (select and record at P2)
- A classic German helles or pilsner as a cleanliness benchmark (select at P2)

## 8. Pilot questions to answer
1. Does an adjunct improve crispness enough to justify itself, or does an all-malt grist win the panel?
2. How long must it lager before it clears the faults list in our equipment (record, do not assume)?
3. What water treatment does Austin water need for a clean, soft finish (lab test first)?
4. Does it hold up to chile heat-level 2-3 dishes without disappearing?
5. Can we hit the clarity and carbonation consistently at tap?

## 9. Pass / fail gate
**PASS:** all faults 0; clean and crisp scored ≥ the reference average by a blind panel; chosen as the better partner than the Hefeweizen for at least two heat-level-2 dishes in pairing trials; consistent across two batches. **FAIL:** rework water, fermentation or lager time; do not mask faults with lime or adjunct.

## 10. What remains the head brewer's professional responsibility
Recipe design (grist, hops, adjuncts), mash and water strategy, yeast selection and pitching, fermentation and conditioning schedules, lager time, carbonation and packaging, sanitation and safety, QC methods and limits, scale-up, TTB/regulatory formula questions, and the decision to proceed or stop. This brief gives the intent and the gates only.
