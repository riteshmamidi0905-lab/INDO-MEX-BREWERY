## 0b. The Hefeweizen gate, restated as a development gate
Not an emotional rule ('if Hefe fails nothing launches'). NASHA does not CLAIM an integrated brewery-and-kitchen program until at least one house beer shows (a) quality, by your written release (faults 0, style-appropriate), repeat batches and a blind panel at or above a named reference beer, and (b) food compatibility, by blind pairings with real dishes (at least 3, per the program's P3 gate). A Hefeweizen failure triggers a root-cause review and a rework by you; it does not stop work on the other beers. Results from one legal pathway are not evidence for another.
**What the Hefeweizen must prove (founder/R&D PROPOSALS, not industry standards; the brewer should set the real thresholds):** (1) credibility: a brewer-released batch, faults 0, scored against a named reference; (2) differentiation: a panel can tell it from the reference in the intended direction (banana/clove balance, haze, foam); (3) repeat drinkability: "would order a second" at or above the reference; (4) food compatibility: it holds up in blind pairings with real dishes (hypotheses today); (5) fermentation reliability: consecutive batches within a tolerance the brewer sets; (6) taproom appeal: haze and foam hold across kegs and the pour reads well.
**Outcomes:** CONTINUE (all six met); REVISE (credibility met but another proof or sample size is not; change one variable and re-brew); DE-PRIORITIZE (the beer is sound but pairing or appeal is weaker than another house beer; keep on tap, not the flagship); REMOVE (repeated faults with no root cause after the brewer's review, or the equipment cannot support the style; a founder decision). Nothing here is sacred because it was listed first: if a brewer says another beer should lead the program, record that as a decision change.
**Known gaps in this handoff that you will notice (open questions, not omissions we are hiding):** no volume split across the five beers; no tap count; no filtration/centrifuge or fining plan (isinglass is excluded for D-54 reasons, alternatives open); no yeast-handling plan for a mixed ale/lager house; no CO2/glycol load; lager and ale days are unsourced assumptions; the tank sizes in the concept images (about 50 bbl) are illustrative only and conflict with much smaller plausible taproom demand: see `beer/BREWERY-SCALE-DECISION-FRAMEWORK.md`. ## 0c. Pilsner vs Mexican-Style Lager: pre-brew differentiation hypothesis
| Dimension | Pilsner (5D) | Mexican-Style Lager (2A / BA nearest) |
|---|---|---|
| Hop expression | floral/herbal noble hops, moderate | faint or none |
| Bitterness | 22-40 style; repo window 28-34 | repo window 14-18 is UNRECONCILED (BJCP 2A 18-25, BA 15-22): if raised the contrast narrows |
| Dryness and finish | crisp, dry, snappy | light, fairly dry, softer finish |
| Malt | bready pale malt | cracker-like; optional corn only if it earns its place |
| Food role (hypothesis) | fried and rich food; bitterness as a reset | heat relief; low bitterness does not amplify chile |
We do not claim more difference than the references support; perceived bitterness also depends on dryness and malt.
**Decision framework (proposal, not tested).** Jobs: the Pilsner is the credibility beer and the reset for fried or rich food; the Mexican-Style Lager is the chile-relief session beer. Minimum difference a normal guest must notice: at least one of hop aroma, bitterness or dryness in a side-by-side, and the difference must matter for the stated use (a triangle test alone shows detectability, not usefulness). If guests cannot tell them apart: rework the specs once and retest; if still the same, one beer is redundant: keep one and make the other a clearly different seasonal, or drop one (a founder decision, with the brewer's view). Operationally both are lagers of roughly 30 days (an UNSOURCED assumption) competing for the same cold tanks, so alternate fills may cut volume to one lager per cycle; if sales split is very uneven one lager plus a hop variant could serve both (needs sales data and brewer input). Statistics: fix n (18-30) and the pass count before tasting, chance is 1/3, never re-run until it passes, state the effect size that matters, add a follow-up (which attribute differs, and would you choose it for the dish). Powering, panel screening and ISO method are for a sensory scientist; ISO 4120 was not read. The Mexican-Style Lager 14-18 IBU window is below the BJCP 2A and Brewers Association ranges and looks chosen to widen the gap: ask the brewer whether to raise it.

## 0d. Future triangle test (design only, NOT RUN)
Question: are the NASHA Pilsner and Mexican-Style Lager perceptibly AND usefully distinct? Panel 18-30 adult lager drinkers (not trained, not involved in the brew), the brewer outside the count; opaque or dark-tinted cups, random 3-digit codes, a neutral server; the six triangle orders balanced equally; 4 C primary arm (a warmer 6-9 C arm as a separate session, because warmth helps noble-hop aroma); 30-60 ml per cup, spitting allowed, unsalted crackers and water, 1-2 minutes between triangles. **Pre-set criterion (one-sided binomial, chance 1/3; fix panel size, alpha and the pass count before tasting and never change them):** minimum correct identifications at alpha 0.05 / 0.10: n=12: 8 / 7; n=18: 10 / 10; n=24: 13 / 12; n=30: 15 / 14 (arithmetic checked with scipy.stats.binomtest (a sensory scientist should confirm the design; ISO 4120 was not read)). A significant triangle shows detectability only; add a descriptive follow-up (which of hop aroma, bitterness, dryness, malt, finish differs and how) and a use question (which with fried food, which with a chile dish); detectably different but not matching the intended roles = partial pass. If distinction fails: rework the specs first (hop aroma, bitterness window, dryness, malt, adjunct), retest, log it; removing either beer is a founder decision, never a silent deletion. Small panel = directional evidence only; it does not transfer to other batches or pathways.
