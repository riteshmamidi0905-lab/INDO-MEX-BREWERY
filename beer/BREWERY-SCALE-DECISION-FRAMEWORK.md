# Brewery scale: decision framework (not a recommendation; no prices, no demand forecast)
**Status:** planning aid prepared with an AI brewer-agent review (not a qualified brewer). The old visual canon of about 50 bbl working tanks and the demand reference of about 780 bbl a year (Brewers Association median 2.6 bbl per seat per year at 300 seats, T3, arithmetic only) do not fit together: one 50 bbl fill would be roughly three to four weeks of that demand, and a 30-day lager cycle would leave tanks idle. The tank size in the concept images is illustrative. **Vessel and brewhouse size must come from a brewer's variety and tank-turn analysis, not from the render.**

## Data needed before choosing (none exists in the repo)
Tap count and beer mix; seats and visit frequency; whether kegs leave the building and whether cans are planned; target annual barrels; lager vs ale share; the brewer's real lager and ale days; local labour rates and utility quotes; sewer and pretreatment limits; floor plan; the legal pathway (attorney).

## Scale ladder, directional only
| Size | Variety | Lagering | Tank turns | Taproom supply | Packaging | Staffing | Floor, utilities, capex |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot | very high | impractical | n/a | cannot supply | none | 1 | unknown |
| 3-5 bbl | high | tight | many | small only | kegs | 1-2 | unknown |
| 7-10 bbl | good | workable | moderate | small-mid | kegs, maybe cans | 2 | unknown |
| 15 bbl | good | workable | moderate | mid | kegs, cans possible | 2-3 | unknown |
| 30 bbl | lower per beer | needs cold tanks | fewer | large | cans plausible | 3+ | unknown |
| 60 bbl | low | needs cold tanks | slow | very large | packaging line likely | 4+ | unknown |
These are reasoning ratings, not data. Utilities (glycol, CO2, steam, drains, pretreatment) need quotes from the brewer and an architect.

## How to decide
1. Fix the real taproom volume range with a brewer and the legal pathway with an attorney. 2. Ask the brewer for fermenter count and size that keep five styles on a rotating schedule (open question: five dedicated fermenters, or sharing). 3. Test the choice against ale/lager scheduling and a pilot-first path. 4. Only then ask vendors for quotes. A larger system should be justified by sales data, not by the building concept.
