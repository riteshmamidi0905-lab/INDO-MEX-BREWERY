# The History Corridor

**Idea:** the guest promenade by the Tank Hall becomes a short, quiet gallery that ends in the spectacular view of the stainless Tank Cloister: PAST → FERMENTATION → PRESENT. `[CONCEPT]`

## Experience (one paragraph)
You step in from the entrance recess into pale limestone and bone terrazzo. A low, dark threshold room says only that there are two worlds and one table. Walking west along the promenade you pass a run of museum-quality glass cases lit at about 2300K: a dried chile of Mexico beside a dried chile of India, a maize cob in four colours, millet panicles, a pressed marigold, a replica silver coin, magnified Harappan starch grains, thin white map lines on dark steel with solid and dashed routes. Each case works without a word; a sentence sits below, a QR opens the sources. Then the walls fall away: the corridor opens to the interior glass wall and the six polished tanks stand in their horseshoe in front of you. You have walked from grain and trade to fermentation to the present.

## Placement and geometry (locked-geometry compatible)
- It **is** the guest promenade: the southern ~12 ft of the Tank Hall, ~44 ft long, with the interior glass wall to the tanks on the north side and the exterior south glass on the south side (Locked Scene DNA).
- **Light and conservation problem:** south glass admits sun. Mitigation: UV-filtering low-E glazing and the 10-ft concrete canopy; specimens in sealed UV-filtered cases or **reproductions/prints** only; museum humidity and light levels `[PRO: conservator]`.
- **Layout:** a free-standing run of cases down the centre of the lobby end (east ~16 ft, with dark partitions behind), thinning to a low glass-top case along the interior glass wall as the Cloister is revealed. Exterior visibility of the tanks through the south glass is **preserved**; the dark threshold sits behind the entrance recess side wall.
- **Accessible:** 60-in clear route, case sill heights 30-36 in for reading, seating bench, tactile labels `[PRO ADA]`.
- **Do not** put children's history here; the Game Hall and Adventure Court carry that.

## Content
Four-level information hierarchy. Cases: (1) Two Worlds, (2) Chile, (3) Maize and nixtamal, (4) Grains of the Indus, (5) The Pacific road, (6) Fermentation. Curated and rotated every season.
