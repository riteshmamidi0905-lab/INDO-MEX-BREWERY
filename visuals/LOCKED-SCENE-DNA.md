Working name only: INDO MEX BREWERY. No logo exists. Concept visual: not an operating business.

## 1. Orientation and the canonical camera position
- North is up in the plan. The entry drive and parking are on the **south**. The "hero camera" for ARC-01 and ARC-02 stands at the **southeast** of the building on the entry drive, looking **north-northwest**, at eye level, about 150-170 ft from the Tank Hall.
- Day light: late afternoon, sun low in the **west-southwest**, i.e. raking from the **camera-left**, so the Tank Hall's south glass is lit and the court arcade throws deep shadows.

## 4. The Tank Cloister (canonical)
- **Six** identical fermenters in a **horseshoe open to the south glass**.
- Numbering (optional on images; omit if the generator cannot render clean numerals): west leg south→north **1, 2**; north base west→east **3, 4** (flanking the brew deck); east leg north→south **5, 6**.
- Each tank: ~5 ft diameter; polished cylindrical shell ~12 ft; **insulated conical bottom clad in matte charcoal**, ~4 ft; four short legs; overall ~19 ft; dished top with top manway, pressure relief/spunding fitting and CIP arm; **round side manway with clamp**; sample valve; thermowell; racking/racking-arm and butterfly valves at the cone outlet; **tri-clamp** connections; restrained, tidy piping on the inner side of the horseshoe.
- **Marigold band:** one narrow (~6 in) marigold-orange band around the middle of each shell, executed as a removable powder-coated sleeve (never paint on a sanitary surface). Brightness restrained; no other orange on tanks.
- Spacing ~9 ft between tank centrelines; ~28 ft clear between the west and east legs.
- **Brew deck:** raised ~4 ft, ~24 ft wide x 12 ft deep, at the centre of the north base between tanks 3 and 4; stairs at each end; 42-in guardrails; **two copper-clad vessels** (kettle and mash/lauter tun, ~7 ft diameter, domed tops, steam stack rising to the roof), stainless hot-liquor tank, pumps, valve train, hoses coiled on hooks.
- **Catwalk:** grated stainless/steel walkway at about +11 ft along the west and east legs with a cross-walk behind the brew deck, 42-in guardrails with kick plates, stairs at each end (access to tank tops).
- **Floor drains:** stainless trench drains along each leg and in front of the brew deck; floor slopes to drains.
- Brewers' door at the north; no guest access to this floor. The south and east glass walls separate guests from production.

## 6. Lighting
- Interior and arcade artificial light: shielded, ~2200-2400K appearance, low. Linear wall-washers at column bases, small shielded downlights in soffits, tank uplights from the floor (narrow beam) so the **tanks are the brightest large objects at night**, a soft glow from the kitchen windows and the enclosed wood-fired oven mouth in the north arcade wall, reflections in the rill, soft uplights in oaks. **No string lights, no neon, no coloured light.**
- Day: hard late-afternoon sun, deep arcade shade, clear dusty-blue sky.

## 11. Cautions (design principle)
- We do not need a cultural story for every object. A floor can just be a good floor.
- Marigold is a visual accent; its historical cultural connection is **not** established. Black stoneware and terrazzo are chosen because they work, not because of unverified shared-tradition claims. Do not publish those claims. Do not print "two cuisines that already traded fire, fruit and flower" anywhere; it is an internal thesis only.
