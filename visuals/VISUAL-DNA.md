> **Cautions (decision D-25):** the "Why" notes below that link marigold, black clay and terrazzo to India/Mexico traditions are *design rationale under verification*, not published claims. Materials stand on their own merit. Do not print the thesis "two cuisines that already traded fire, fruit and flower" on signage or in copy yet.


Goal: from a single photograph, with no logo, someone should know this is the same place. The system avoids the generic "terracotta + brass + geometric tile + agave + lattice" look. Each rule has a *reason* rooted in research or operations.

## The ten non-negotiable rules
1. **THE TANK CLOISTER.** Six polished stainless conical fermenters stand in a **horseshoe** in a double-height glazed hall. Every exterior/interior image that shows the hall shows this horseshoe. Cones are insulated and clad in **matte charcoal**; shells are polished stainless; each tank has large white stencil numbers (1-6) and a visible manway. *Why:* the tanks are the icon (founder directive) and the horseshoe is the architectural idea.
2. **ONE SATURATED COLOR: MARIGOLD.** A single narrow **marigold-orange band** is painted around the middle of every tank, and the same orange appears *only* in small, deliberate places (tile stripe, can band, apron stitch, napkin stripe). Never terracotta, never brass-gold. *Why:* marigold is native to Mexico and central in Indian ritual and decoration `[V S-46 partial]`; it is a real shared plant, not a symbol of either culture's stereotype.
3. **MATERIAL PALETTE (no more):** pale Texas limestone, board-formed concrete, blackened steel, polished stainless, copper-clad kettles (real brewing metal), pecan or cedar-elm timber. No brass fixtures, no terracotta walls, no mosaic-pattern tile.
4. **TERRAZZO FLOOR:** speckled terrazzo (granito) in bone with red-black volcanic aggregate, polished matte, throughout the interior. *Why:* terrazzo/granito flooring is a shared tradition `[U]`; tezontle is Mexican volcanic stone `[U]`.
5. **THE BAR:** long dark pecan top, front clad in **square 4-inch handmade matte tiles in only two colors: bone and marigold**, set as a simple broken stripe; blackened steel footrail; 24 tap handles in matte black with a single marigold ring. Backbar: open steel shelving with the glassware family and the tank hall visible through glass behind.
6. **THE COURT:** a deep (12-15 ft) arcade of board-formed concrete columns with timber beams on three sides, **perforated blackened-steel screens** whose perforations are *rings* (derived from the tank plan, not stock lattice); a shallow stone rill; live oaks and cedar elms; large ceiling fans; communal pecan tables with stainless inlay; no string-light canopy as the main lighting.
7. **LIGHT:** all artificial light warm (2200-2400K), low, and shielded; tanks softly uplit from the floor; fire (tandoor mouth, comal glow) visible in the kitchen. Day scenes: hard Texas sun with deep shade, never overcast fantasy. Night scenes: indigo sky, amber glow, tanks the brightest objects.
8. **TABLEWARE SYSTEM:** matte **black stoneware** plates and bowls with a raw unglazed rim and bone-white interior; small **stainless katori** bowls for sauces; round stainless **thali trays** lined with bone paper; indigo-and-bone linen napkins with one marigold stripe; flight boards in pecan with four round cut-outs. *Why:* both Oaxaca (barro negro) and India (e.g., Nizamabad, Longpi) have black-clay pottery traditions `[U]`; black stoneware makes food photograph richly.
9. **THE GLASS:** the **hero Hefeweizen glass** is a tall 0.5 L weizen vase with a **raised glass ring (collar)** about one-quarter of the way up the base, a fine rim, and a thick clear base. Lager glass: tall conical lager glass with the same collar. Reserve glass: short stemmed goblet with a stepped bowl. Flight glass: 4 oz tulip with the collar. *Why:* the ring echoes the tank band and is photographable; performance details (rim, volume, taper) follow weizen glass practice.
10. **PEOPLE AND TONE:** mixed ages and backgrounds, relaxed, candid, unnamed, mid-conversation; staff in charcoal canvas aprons over bone shirts with marigold stitching; brewers in black work shirts and rubber boots. No costumes, no turbans/sombreros/saris as props, no mandala or deity imagery, no faux-Devanagari, no flags, no stock-photo smiles. All images are labeled *Concept visual: not an operating business.*

## Photography language
- **Architecture:** 24-35mm equivalent, eye level or slightly lower, vertical lines corrected; golden hour or blue hour.
- **Interiors:** 24-35mm, f/5.6-8, tripod feel, practical lights visible.
- **Beer:** 85-100mm macro, f/2.8-4, backlit, condensation realistic, foam dense, correct pour level (about 3-4 cm head).
- **Food:** 85-105mm, 30-45° or overhead, side-lit window light, natural char, real sauce drips, slight asymmetry, steam only where plausible.
- **People:** 35-50mm, shallow depth, candid motion blur acceptable.

## Brand restrictions
No logos unless an approved concept is supplied; where signage appears use blank blackened steel or the placeholder wordmark text "INDO MEX" only if specified. Cans carry **patterns and bands but no readable text** (add typography in post). Avoid real brand marks (Corona, Kingfisher, etc.).

## Realism checklist for reviewing generated images
Tank count = 6 in the horseshoe; marigold band present; charcoal cones; terrazzo floor; collar on glass; black stoneware on food; no brass; no terracotta; correct hand anatomy; cheese stretches plausibly; beer has head and haze; shadows consistent with time of day.
