# Fermentation History (for the Grain & Fermentation Wall and the Cloister approach)

**Principle:** we do not say who "discovered" fermentation; we show the oldest evidence we have and parallel living traditions.

## Evidence we can use
- **Earliest chemical evidence of a fermented drink:** a mixed rice, honey and fruit beverage at Jiahu, China, c. 7000 BCE (McGovern et al., PNAS 2004; reached via S-65, a secondary source). Whether the fruit was grape or hawthorn is uncertain. `[VERIFIED, secondary]`
- **Early barley beer in Iran:** residues c. 3400 BCE (single secondary source) `[SUPPORTED → verify]`. A "13,000-year-old Haifa beer" claim is **DO NOT USE**.
- **Weihenstephan brewery licensed in Freising in 1040;** it claims to be the world's oldest existing brewery (Weltenburg claims 1050) `[VERIFIED S-43a]`.
- **Witbier:** the style nearly disappeared (last Hoegaarden brewery closed between 1955 and 1960 depending on the source), was revived by Pierre Celis, and he later founded Celis in Austin in 1992 `[VERIFIED S-42a]`.
- **Living traditions:** Northeast India's rice beers (apong, zutho, others) `[VERIFIED S-74a]`; tesgüino, a maize beer made by Uto-Aztecan peoples and sacred to the Tarahumara `[VERIFIED S-74b]`. These are parallel inventions with living communities: consult them.
- **Mexican cuisine's foundation includes nixtamalization** (UNESCO, S-66), a transformation of grain through chemistry, closely analogous to malting as a grain-transformation technique `[C: our interpretation]`.

## Unresolved / not yet researched
Pulque and agave fermentation history; Egyptian and Mesopotamian beer in depth; the Indus evidence for fermented drinks; Bavarian wheat-beer history (Schneider's claims were blog-only); bread leavening history; fermented foods (idli, dosa, kanji, cheese, tepache).

## Exhibit arc (Grain & Fermentation Wall → Tank Cloister)
ANCIENT GRAIN (barley, wheat, maize, rice, millets as specimens) → TRANSFORMATION (malting, nixtamalization, fermentation) → LIVING TRADITIONS (apong, tesgüino with consultation) → BREWING EVOLUTION (Weihenstephan 1040; witbier revival) → MODERN CRAFT → **our concept Tank Cloister** (a concept, not an operating brewery).
