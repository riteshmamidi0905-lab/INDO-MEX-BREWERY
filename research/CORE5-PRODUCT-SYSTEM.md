**Findings.** Representation is balanced (two vegetarian, three meat, no beef or pork). Spice and richness range is adequate but the set leans rich (goat, quesadilla, 65 are all high-fat) and the only fresh/acid counterweights are Paneer and Gobi. Cooking methods are varied (fry, char, braise, roast, griddle) which is a strength for distinctiveness and a cost for the kitchen. The Indian side of Goat Birria is the weakest (technique only); the Mexican side of Gobi Taco is modern, not traditional (no claim of a Mexican precedent). Mashup risk is highest on Paneer Tikka Taco and the Quesadilla (two cuisines side by side); the strongest crossings are those where a *technique* crosses (sealed-pot braise, masa chip as the plate).
**Risks, not changes.** The Core 5 is unchanged. ## 2. Per-dish R&D hypotheses (all ANALYSIS; a chef decides)
- **Chicken 65 Totopos:** frozen Session 1 (D-74) is authoritative; nothing here modifies it. Science for Session 2+: moisture migration into the chip is the expected failure; candidates to test after Session 1 are sauce beside vs on the chip, thick vs thin sauce, an oil-phase glaze, a hydrocolloid pre-dust. Log chip thickness, moisture/water activity, oil temperature, fry time, hold time (research/EXTERNAL-INTELLIGENCE-S15.md section 2; L-001..L-005).
- **Paneer Tikka Taco (Session 2):** about 2 cm cubes, thick hung-curd marinade with a little cooled mustard oil, kasuri methi, chaat masala, optional besan (a variable), marinate 1-4 h; char over very high heat to order; do not overcook (rubbery paneer is mostly overcooking and moisture loss, T1 blogs); corn tortilla 12-15 cm; salsa verde, raw onion, cilantro, lime; pineapple optional and never called al pastor. Test starting point 45-70 g paneer (3-4 cubes) per taco. **One variable for Session 2: heat source** (tandoor if obtainable, else charbroiler/comal, labelled as substitute). Measure: cook endpoint vs char area and tenderness; coating retained; hold at 0/5/10 min. India-yes/Mexico-yes/NASHA-yes recorded after tasting, not before. Founder must freeze the thresholds in sheet 03 section 9b before cooking.
- **Goat Birria:** reference first: Jalisco birria de chivo (bone-in goat, guajillo-ancho adobo, vinegar, spices, braised, consomme, corn tortillas, onion, cilantro, lime, salsa). NASHA layer limited to technique that should improve it: sealed pot, whole-spice toasting, bhuna-frying the adobo paste. Yogurt-papaya marinade and curry-leaf tadka are *tested against a plain-adobo control*, not assumed. No cheese by default; keep bones and fibre visible so it reads as goat, not beef quesabirria. First physical step: a 1-2 kg pilot (plain vs Indian-layer adobo; yield, trim, skim, 60/120 min hold; blind vs lamb taco).
- **Gobi Taco:** roast-first florets with stems attached (blanch about 3 min, steam-dry, thin yogurt-besan film, hot roast, late tamarind-guajillo glaze) as the primary; rice-flour batter fried as the comparison. Avoid the fried-chicken read (no uniform craggy coating, no round nuggets). A test sheet does not exist yet and must be written before Session 3. Measure crunch at 0/5/10/15 min; glaze grams vs tortilla softening.
- **Butter Chicken & Roasted Poblano Quesadilla:** reduce the makhani until it coats a spoon, chill the filling, charred chicken about 1 cm dice, cheese against both tortilla faces as a barrier, rajas against the cheese, dry comal medium heat, rest 1-2 min. Test starting point 40-50 g cheese, 50-70 g chicken-sauce, 20-30 g rajas in a 25 cm tortilla. One variable first: sauce thickness vs leak minutes. Allergens: dairy certain, wheat if flour, cashew only if used. Butter chicken origin is contested in court (Moti Mahal vs Daryaganj, T1): never pick one.

## 5. The 5 x 5 pairing hypotheses (NOT SENSORY TESTED)
`tools/pairing_matrix.py` scores 25 pairings on nine 0-3 dimensions (heat relief, richness cut, carbonation role, char compatibility, malt bridge, acid interaction, spice interaction, body match, intensity match) from attribute columns in the two matrices. These are crude rule-of-thumb heuristics; their only use is choosing what to taste first. The tool assigns PRIMARY by an optimal one-to-one assignment so every beer has a job (a coverage design choice, shown beside the unconstrained best), SECONDARY = best remaining, EXPERIMENTAL = a contrast pick.
| Dish | Heuristic assignment (primary) | Heuristic secondary | Heuristic experimental | Unconstrained top | Agent reasoning (advice from AI specialist agents, not a professional's opinion) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken 65 Totopos | Mexican-Style Lager | Hefeweizen | Witbier | Hefeweizen | chef agent: Mexican Lager vs Witbier vs Hefeweizen on one fried batch; brewer agent: Mexican Lager vs Pilsner as a heat-contrast test |
| Paneer Tikka Taco | Witbier | Hefeweizen | Vienna Amber | Witbier | chef and brewer agents: Pilsner vs Witbier (check that bitterness plus char does not turn acrid) |
| Goat Birria | Hefeweizen | Vienna Amber | Witbier | Hefeweizen | chef agent: Mexican Lager vs Vienna vs Hefeweizen (clove against clove/cinnamon is a congruence gamble); brewer agent: Vienna vs Hefeweizen |
| Gobi Taco | Pilsner | Witbier | Hefeweizen | Witbier | chef agent: Witbier vs Pilsner after fry vs roast is settled; brewer agent: Witbier, plus Pilsner as a heat test |
| Butter Chicken Quesadilla | Vienna Amber | Hefeweizen | Witbier | Hefeweizen | chef agent: Witbier or Pilsner against Vienna (Vienna may cloy on a sweet cream sauce); brewer agent: Vienna vs Hefeweizen |
**Disagreement recorded.** The chef agent found the heuristic does not discriminate: the Hefeweizen is the unconstrained top for three dishes and the spread between candidates is 0.1-0.3 of a mean score (about one rounding step), so the model cannot rank pairings; its 'primary' is a coverage assignment (every beer gets a job), not the best pairing. Attribute values were corrected after the chef and brewer agent critiques (carbonation scaled to CO2, Chicken 65 fried raised to 4, Gobi heat 2, hop aroma added, and so on) before re-running; they were not tuned to reach an answer. The agents also disagree with the heuristic about specific dishes (for example Vienna for goat, Witbier or Pilsner for paneer). **The first tasting should therefore taste the UNION of all lists, not any one of them.** **Proposed first pairing tastings (10 of about 17 candidates, after the foods and beers physically exist):** C65+Mexican Lager, C65+Hefeweizen, C65+Pilsner (deliberate heat-contrast), Paneer+Witbier, Paneer+Pilsner, Goat+Vienna, Goat+Hefeweizen, Gobi+Witbier (after fry vs roast), Quesadilla+Hefeweizen, Quesadilla+Vienna. A dish can pair with several beers and a beer with several dishes; discovery is the point. Gobi pairings assume the roast arm.
**Two-Pour (one dish, two small pours).** Conceptual only. Sensory value: guests could taste how beer changes the food. Cautions from the brand review: it risks normalising two drinks per dish and building "always two"; "Two-Pour" already names the Hefeweizen pour technique (two words, two meanings); the name NASHA can mean intoxication so a "ritual" frame is risky. Proposed handling (founder to decide, not applied): one small pour by default with the second optional, an equal non-alcoholic pour, rename the pairing offer (for example "pairing pour"), keep "Two-Pour" for the Hefeweizen technique only, attorney and server-training review.

**Can five dishes run from a compact kitchen without five kitchens?
