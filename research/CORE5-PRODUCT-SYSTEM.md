The Indian side of Goat Birria is the weakest (technique only); the Mexican side of Gobi Taco is modern, not traditional (no claim of a Mexican precedent). ## 2. Per-dish R&D hypotheses (all ANALYSIS; a chef decides)
- **Chicken 65 Totopos:** frozen Session 1 (D-74) is authoritative; nothing here modifies it. Science for Session 2+: moisture migration into the chip is the expected failure; candidates to test after Session 1 are sauce beside vs on the chip, thick vs thin sauce, an oil-phase glaze, a hydrocolloid pre-dust. Log chip thickness, moisture/water activity, oil temperature, fry time, hold time (research/EXTERNAL-INTELLIGENCE-S15.md section 2; L-001..L-005).
- **Paneer Tikka Taco (Session 2):** about 2 cm cubes, thick hung-curd marinade with a little cooled mustard oil, kasuri methi, chaat masala, optional besan (a variable), marinate 1-4 h; char over very high heat to order; do not overcook (rubbery paneer is mostly overcooking and moisture loss, T1 blogs); corn tortilla 12-15 cm; salsa verde, raw onion, cilantro, lime; pineapple optional and never called al pastor. Test starting point 45-70 g paneer (3-4 cubes) per taco. **One variable for Session 2: heat source** (tandoor if obtainable, else charbroiler/comal, labelled as substitute). Measure: cook endpoint vs char area and tenderness; coating retained; hold at 0/5/10 min. India-yes/Mexico-yes/NASHA-yes recorded after tasting, not before. Founder must freeze the thresholds in sheet 03 section 9b before cooking.
- **Goat Birria:** reference first: Jalisco birria de chivo (bone-in goat, guajillo-ancho adobo, vinegar, spices, braised, consomme, corn tortillas, onion, cilantro, lime, salsa). NASHA layer limited to technique that should improve it: sealed pot, whole-spice toasting, bhuna-frying the adobo paste. Yogurt-papaya marinade and curry-leaf tadka are *tested against a plain-adobo control*, not assumed. No cheese by default; keep bones and fibre visible so it reads as goat, not beef quesabirria. First physical step: a 1-2 kg pilot (plain vs Indian-layer adobo; yield, trim, skim, 60/120 min hold; blind vs lamb taco).
- **Gobi Taco:** roast-first florets with stems attached (blanch about 3 min, steam-dry, thin yogurt-besan film, hot roast, late tamarind-guajillo glaze) as the primary; rice-flour batter fried as the comparison. Avoid the fried-chicken read (no uniform craggy coating, no round nuggets). A test sheet does not exist yet and must be written before Session 3. Measure crunch at 0/5/10/15 min; glaze grams vs tortilla softening.
- **Butter Chicken & Roasted Poblano Quesadilla:** reduce the makhani until it coats a spoon, chill the filling, charred chicken about 1 cm dice, cheese against both tortilla faces as a barrier, rajas against the cheese, dry comal medium heat, rest 1-2 min. Test starting point 40-50 g cheese, 50-70 g chicken-sauce, 20-30 g rajas in a 25 cm tortilla. One variable first: sauce thickness vs leak minutes. Allergens: dairy certain, wheat if flour, cashew only if used. Butter chicken origin is contested in court (Moti Mahal vs Daryaganj, T1): never pick one.

## 5. The 5 x 5 pairing hypotheses (NOT SENSORY TESTED)
**Pairing system V2 (Sprint 17).** Structural logic and sensory validation are now separate. `tools/pairing_matrix.py` (v2) no longer scores 25 pairings numerically: the v1 heuristic could not discriminate (Hefeweizen topped three dishes by 0.1-0.3 of a mean score, one rounding step), so its numbers were false precision and were dropped. It now derives, from the 0-5 style-typical attribute columns in the two matrices, **mechanism tags** (CONTRAST, HEAT, BRIDGE, ECHO, ACID, SWEET, BODY) and **risk flags** per food-beer pair into `food/CORE5-PAIRING-MATRIX.csv` (status PAIRING HYPOTHESIS - NOT SENSORY TESTED). Mechanism = why a pairing might work; risk = how it might fail; neither says a pairing works at the table.
**Smallest useful first flight** (`food/CORE5-PAIRING-FLIGHT-1.csv`, from a sensory-scientist agent, advice not evidence): 6 CORE pairings in two sittings plus 2 OPTIONAL: PF-01 Chicken 65 + Mexican-Style Lager, PF-02 Gobi (roast arm) + Pilsner (deliberate heat-contrast counter-test), PF-03 Paneer + Witbier (sitting 1); PF-04 Quesadilla + Vienna, PF-05 Goat + Hefeweizen, PF-06 Goat + Vienna (sitting 2, same goat portion); optional PF-07 Chicken 65 + Pilsner (also a Pilsner vs Mexican Lager role check), PF-08 Quesadilla + Hefeweizen (tests the v1 unconstrained top). Conditions: at most four pairings per sitting, 60 ml pours, water and plain cracker between, order randomised, pairings need a cooked food and a stand-in beer (a commercial beer of the same style until a NASHA beer exists), and Chicken 65 pairing is a separate event from D-74 Session 1 (it does not touch the frozen rule).
**Scoring sheet per pairing (1-5):** food improved by the beer; beer improved by the food; balance; would order again; tick mechanisms noticed (CONTRAST, HEAT, BRIDGE, ECHO, ACID, SWEET, BODY); overall helped / neutral / hurt. n under 8 is descriptive only; do not average into a rank.
**Disagreements preserved.** The chef agent disliked any single "primary" per dish (the old coverage assignment was a design choice, not a result); the brewer agent expected Vienna for goat and Pilsner or Witbier for paneer; they disagree with each other and with the rule on several pairs. The flight tests the union of their picks, not one list. Gobi pairings assume the roast arm.
**Two-Pour evaluation (idea, not applied).** Sensory value: tasting how beer changes food. Risks from the brand review: normalises two drinks per dish and builds "always two"; "Two-Pour" already names the Hefeweizen pour technique; NASHA can mean intoxication, so a "ritual" frame is risky. Recommended handling (founder decides): one small pour by default, a second optional, an equal-standing non-alcoholic alternative, and if the name misleads consider a plain label such as "pairing pour" (not renamed publicly). Keep "Two-Pour" for the Hefeweizen technique only; attorney and server-training review.
**Non-alcoholic program, strategic role.** It is not a courtesy list: it is the second half of the pairing system (each dish gets an equal-standing alcohol-free pour so a table of mixed drinkers can all take part), it protects the responsible-alcohol stance, and it widens who can join a Two-Pour table. It is also untested; recipes, costs and regulations (labeling, any dealcoholized product rules) are open.

**Can five dishes run from a compact kitchen without five kitchens? Unproven.** Plausible only around three heat zones: one fryer, one griddle/comal bank, one oven or kettle for the braise; the tandoor is substitutable (charbroiler/comal) and is not needed to start. Bottlenecks: marinades for three dishes all land the day before service; chile rehydration and sauce prep collide on one shift; oil sharing across chicken, masa and cauliflower needs flavour/allergen carryover review; a mixed ticket (65 + quesadilla + birria) puts three hot stations on one cook. Austin hood/fire rules and tandoor throughput/safety were not found.
**Format fit (inference, attorney/agency confirmation needed):** pop-up: Chicken 65, Gobi (if roasted or fried), Paneer on a grill/comal; brewery residency: Chicken 65, Paneer, Quesadilla, Goat only if prepared in a permitted commissary; trailer: Chicken 65, Gobi, Quesadilla (no tandoor, no long braise; Austin mobile vending moving to a DSHS permit, V-70); permanent kitchen: all five (tandoor subject to the hood/fire answer); flagship: all five plus consomme service. A flagship item does not belong in every format.
**Goat Birria MVP risks:** long braise, low yield and trim waste, goat sourcing/seasonality/cost, large-batch cooling and reheating and hold (food-safety professional and health department set the process; local rules not researched), fat setting in consomme, tortilla fragility after dipping, gaminess varying by animal.
