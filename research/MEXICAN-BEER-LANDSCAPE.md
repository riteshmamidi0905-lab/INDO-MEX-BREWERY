# Mexican Beer Landscape

## A. Historic brewing
- German and Austrian brewers shaped Mexican brewing, with Vienna lager taking root around the 1860s Maximilian era (sources disagree on his direct role). By the early 20th century 35+ independent breweries existed; consolidation into two giants followed. `[V S-08,S-09,S-10]`
- **Cuauhtémoc** (Monterrey, ~1890-91, Carta Blanca); **Moctezuma** (Orizaba, Veracruz; Hasse; Siglo XX, now Dos Equis); merged 1985; Heineken-owned since 2010. **Bohemia** (1905), Pilsner-style. `[V S-10,S-49c]` (dates vary by source)
- **Modelo** (Mexico City): founding date 1922 or 1925 depending on source; **Negra Modelo** is a **Munich Dunkel-style** lager (5.4% ABV), first draught 1926; Dos Equis Ámbar is a **Vienna-style amber** (4.7% ABV). `[V S-49a,b,c]` This corrects our first draft, which implied Negra Modelo was Vienna.
- **Why it developed this way `[C]`:** European immigrant brewers, lager technology and refrigeration arriving together; warm climate favoring light, cold-served lager; amber and dark lagers surviving as "special" styles.

## B. Modern craft
- Craft is often traced to Gustavo Rodríguez (Cosaco, Mexico City, 1999). A boom followed ~2013 after a competition-regulator action opened retail access (per Minerva's founder). `[V S-26e/d]`
- Notable names in sources: **Cervecería Minerva** (Guadalajara/Zapopan, 2004, founder Jesús Briseño, studied at Siebel), **Primus** (Mexico City; Tempus series 2007), **Calavera** (Tlalnepantla, 2009), **Rámuri** (Tijuana; Bucéfalo Imperial Stout with Mexican coffee), Boca Negra, Colima, Beer Factory (2015 Expansión). `[V S-26a,b,c]` (all dated; status unchecked)
- Scale: craft <1% of market (older sources); Acermex estimates ~2,000 craft breweries produced 330,000 hl in 2022 (the figure includes tiny operators; treat cautiously). `[V S-26e, dated]`
- Events: Expo Cerveza (Guadalajara, Mexico City), Ensenada Beer Fest. `[V S-26e]`
- Oaxaca described as an emerging craft-beer capital (VinePair). `[V S-26e, not read in detail]`
- Ingredients: cacao, regional fruit, chile; hibiscus + agave used by a San Diego brewer (Border X) to balance tartness. `[V S-26a, S-26e]`
