## 3. Cross-cutting quality standards (derived `[C]`, grounded in the above)
1. **Spice layering:** whole spices bloomed in fat, ground spices added at the right moment, acid and fresh herb at the end. `[U]` cite a technique source (e.g., a cookbook or chef interview).
2. **Fire:** tandoor and charcoal grilling produce char, smoke and moisture control that a pan cannot. `[V S-16 for Gymkhana; U general]`
3. **Fermentation:** idli/dosa batter, pickles, curd, kanji, toddy. Treat as texture and acid, a natural bridge to beer. `[U]`
4. **Vegetarian depth:** treat vegetables as the centre, not substitutes. `[U]`
5. **Regional honesty:** each dish carries a region name and a reason.
6. **Service as hospitality, not costume.**

## 4. Chicken 65 (dish study, required for the signature)
- **Origin:** most often attributed to A. M. Buhari of Hotel Buhari, Chennai, 1965; "none have been proven to be fact". `[V S-32a,b]`
- **Name theories (all unproven):** year 1965; soldiers' meal; 65 chillies; 65-day-old chicken (dismissed as unrealistic); 65 pieces; 65 spices. `[V S-32a,b]`
- **Core profile:** chicken marinated in yogurt, ginger, garlic, red chile and spices, deep-fried, garnished with curry leaves and green chillies; sources disagree on bone-in vs boneless. `[V S-32a,b]`
- **Hyderabad:** common in Hyderabad, adapted to local palate. `[V S-32b]`
- **What must survive (identity checklist for a Chicken 65 totopos dish) `[C]`:** (1) boneless, small-cut thigh meat with crisp, deeply red, shaggy-edged crust; (2) ginger-garlic and chile heat with a **sour edge** (yogurt/lemon); (3) fried curry leaves and green chile on top; (4) served dry/tossed, not drowned in sauce. **What the Mexican side adds:** fresh-fried masa totopos, a tomatillo-or-tamarind salsa on the side, pickled onion; cheese optional and *not* on the chicken.
