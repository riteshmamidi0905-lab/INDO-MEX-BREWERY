## 2. Chain A: crispness (question, authoritative research, classification, experiment recommendation, ledger)
Question: what governs crispness loss in fried corn products under moist, hot toppings? `/deep-research` is reserved for explicit user invocation and cannot be run by the agent; the founder may run it. Equivalent inputs gathered instead: Consensus (5 peer-reviewed abstracts, `LITERATURE-LEDGER.csv`), a food-scientist web-search pass (T1, secondary/patent/thesis) and V-63.
Findings (T1, none specific to chicken + crema): chips lose crispness as moisture rises (water activity above roughly 0.35-0.5 per a secondary citation of Katz & Labuza; glass transition also matters); a moisture barrier between moist and dry components reduced crispness loss of nachos under tomato sauce over 50 min (L-001); post-fry crispy-to-soggy change is linked to crust glass transition and holding conditions (L-002); oil temperature raises moisture-loss rate and final oil uptake (L-004); an oil-based sauce or high-solids viscous sauce is claimed (patents, not tested) to wet less; heat-lamp holding dries but does not keep crispness; rice flour has low adhesion and lower oil uptake than wheat batter (secondary).
NOT FOUND: any controlled study of chips under hot protein and sauce; a moisture-migration rate; a thickness-to-sogginess rate; a rice vs cornstarch vs wheat comparison for chicken.
Recommendation (Session 2+ only; Session 1 and D-74 are frozen): log (1) minutes from topping to texture check, chip thickness (mm) and a bare-chip control, (2) chip moisture or water activity before topping and at the check plus oil temperature and fry time, (3) chicken core temperature, coating flour type and pickup, hold time, hold temperature, vented or racked. Test variables worth a pre-registered Session 2: sauce on vs beside the chip, thick vs thin sauce, oil-phase glaze, a hydrocolloid pre-dust (chef decision). Findings explain results after a cook; they predict no score.

## 3. Chain B: Austin market (observed vs inferred)
OBSERVED (sample of Yelp results, 49 listings): by group (median Yelp rating / median review count / price tier mode): Brewery 4.46 / 310 / $$ (n=10); Brewpub 4.35 / 281 / $$ (n=5); Beer garden 3.96 / 326 / $$ (n=4); Indian 4.33 / 478 / $$ (n=8); Mexican 4.30 / 472 / $$ (n=12); other/fusion-ish 4.37 / 1005 / $$ (n=10). Only 5 of 49 listings pair a brewery/brewpub/beer-garden category with a food category (Lazarus: tacos; Nomadic: sandwiches; Brewtorium: traditional American; Oasis: sandwiches/burgers; Draught House: brewpub+beer garden); three listings carry a food-truck tag among beer venues (Zilker, Cosmic) and two among Mexican/Indian (Birrieria Mama Rosa, Pav Bhaji Express, The Urban Turban). `python3 tools/competitor_summary.py` reproduces this.
INFERRED (not established): no listing in these results was categorised as Indian-Mexican fusion. **Sprint 17 verification (T1 snippets, `ops/data/austin-deep-dives.csv`): The Urban Turban (Round Rock) IS a real halal Mexican-Pakistani-American fusion food truck** (tandoori tacos, birria and chicken-tikka quesadillas); no brewery link was found and it is not a direct competitor on current evidence; its birria is likely beef, so it is not a model under D-54. Absence of an Indian-Mexican category in Yelp never proved absence of a concept. Absence from 9 capped searches is not absence from the market. Yelp ratings are user aggregates, not revenue, quality or share; nothing about demand for NASHA is established. Geospatial analysis (GeoPandas, `tools/trade_area.py`) cannot run because the connector returns no coordinates and geocoding hosts are blocked; competitor review themes were not analysed because no review text is returned.
