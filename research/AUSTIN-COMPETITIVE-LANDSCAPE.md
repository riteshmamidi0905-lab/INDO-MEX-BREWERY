## 1. Market context `[V S-27a,b]`
- Texas breweries: **440 (2024) to 420 (2025)** per Brewers Association data cited by Axios (28 Aug 2026). Openings fell below 25/yr in 2024 and 2025 vs 50 openings/30 closures in 2023; at least 25 closures in each of 2024-25. Causes: capital cost, interest rates, real estate and ingredient costs, tight bank lending.
- North Austin (Domain area) brewery count fell from seven to three in about a year (KXAN): oversaturation, rent, overhead, changing tastes. Oskar Blues' Austin taproom closed (date unconfirmed).
- **Implication:** Austin is not under-served in generic breweries; it is **consolidating**. Differentiation and food/experience matter more than another taproom, and capital discipline is essential.

## 3. What does Austin NOT already have? `[C]` (needs customer validation)
Austin has: IPA-led breweries, taco-and-beer neighborhood spots, German halls, farm breweries, many patios with string lights, food trucks.
Austin does **not** appear to have (in what we found):
1. A **cuisine-led brewpub** where the kitchen has a *stated regional logic* as rigorous as the brewhouse (not tacos-with-craft-beer, not pizza).
2. **Architecture as the attraction**: a designed hall where **the brewhouse is the centrepiece**, rather than a converted shed with a bar.
3. A **wheat-beer-and-lager-first** house in a market tilted to IPA, with a hefeweizen-led serve ritual.
4. A **vegetarian-strong** menu in a brewery (Indian cooking enables this natively).
5. **Climate-engineered outdoor space** (deep shade, air movement, water) built for a 100°F city, rather than umbrellas.
These are hypotheses: test with 30+ customer interviews, competitor tasting, and a pop-up.

## 4. Positioning (revised) `[C]`
X = beer rigor; Y = culinary specificity; Z = architectural distinction. Competitors cluster high-beer/low-food-specificity (Pinthouse, Live Oak) or mid/mid (Lazarus). Opportunity: **high × high × high**, at brewpub (not production-brewery) scale, with controlled capital.

## 5. Warning
The same data that shows a gap also shows lenders are cautious. A visible-brewhouse building is a premium-capex concept; the business case must prove food/beverage margin and events revenue (Phase Four).
