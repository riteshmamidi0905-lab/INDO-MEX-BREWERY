## 3. Technique study (all `[C]` standards built from sourced facts)
- **Nixtamalization:** alkaline cooking of corn in calcium hydroxide; the word comes from Nahuatl; softens the kernel, improves flavor and niacin availability; typical practice ≈1% lime by corn weight, 6-12 h steep. `[V S-34, general walk-through]`
- **Heirloom corn:** importers such as Masienda source non-GMO heirloom varieties from Mexican farmers. `[V S-34]` Our plan: in-house nixtamal for totopos/tortillas **if** volume justifies; start with a vetted heirloom masa supplier. `[TEST]`
- **Flour tortillas:** Sonora/northern Mexico tradition; Spanish-introduced wheat; soft white wheat gives sweet, stretchy, very thin large tortillas. `[V S-48a,b]` This is our legitimate bridge to **naan/roti/paratha** (wheat flatbreads).
- **Birria:** regional name in Jalisco for a barbacoa-type dish, traditionally goat, chile adobo marinade, cooked in broth; beef version and consommé tradition developed in Tijuana; quesabirria (cheese, griddled taco, consommé) popularized in North America from Tijuana in the 2010s; origin stories (16th-century goats) are folklore. `[V S-33a,b]`
- **Al pastor:** descends from Lebanese shawarma brought by immigrants (late 19th/early 20th c.), first as *tacos árabes* in Puebla; later pork, achiote, pineapple; dates/restaurants disputed. `[V S-37]` This is **the proof that Mexican cuisine has always absorbed migrant techniques**.
- **Salsas, moles, chiles, agave, seafood, barbacoa:** `[U]` still need individual sources (backlog).

## 4. Birria study for the Indian-influence dish (required)
Identity checklist (what must survive) `[C]`: (1) dried-chile adobo (guajillo/ancho/pasilla family) as the flavor spine; (2) long braise producing a **drinkable consommé**; (3) tortilla dipped and griddled; (4) onion, cilantro, lime on the side; (5) goat is the Jalisco original, beef the Tijuana/US form.
Where Indian goat cooking can contribute without erasing birria `[C]`:
- **Technique** over spice: Hyderabadi/North-Indian marinades use yogurt, ginger-garlic, raw papaya (tenderizer) and long dum (sealed) cooking `[V S-35, C grade]`. A **yogurt-and-papaya pre-marinade** for goat could improve tenderness before the chile adobo goes on. `[TEST]`
- **Fat and onion discipline:** slow-browned onion base (birista-style) deepening the consommé. `[TEST]`
- **Spice restraint:** the adobo stays chile-led; Indian whole spices (cardamom, black pepper, bay, mace) at *bay-leaf* levels, not curry levels. `[TEST]`
- **Finish:** a tableside *tadka* of ghee, cumin and curry leaf over the consommé (sizzle is theater with a purpose). `[C/TEST]`
- This is "Dum Goat Birria": the cooking *logic* (low, sealed, marinade-first braise) is shared, which is the honest connection.
