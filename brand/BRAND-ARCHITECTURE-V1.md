## 2. Personality and tone
Personality: craft-serious, warm, curious, grounded, respectful, confident-not-loud. **Tone:** plain, specific, present tense; explains without lecturing; dry humour allowed, puns about either culture are not. **Voice test:** would a Bavarian brewer and a Hyderabadi cook both nod, and would a nine-year-old understand the menu line? Examples: "Brewed here. Poured at 5 °C." · "Heat 2 of 3. The raita-crema cools it." · "Our own dish, inspired by tandoori marinades and Sonoran grilling."

## 3. Visual direction (summary; rules in `visuals/VISUAL-DNA.md`)
Contemporary hospitality design with intelligent cultural integration: pale limestone, board-formed concrete, blackened steel, polished stainless, copper, pecan/cedar elm, bone terrazzo; **marigold used narrowly**; warm shielded light; fire visible; the ring/collar as the recurring object (glass, tank sleeve, tap-handle ring, can band). The identity must not read as a generic Indian restaurant, Mexican cantina, sports bar or industrial brewery. Wordmark/logo, colour system and type are **not designed yet** (Phase 4).

## 4. Naming hierarchy: three architecture options (decide after trademark work)
| Option | Structure | For | Against | Evidence that would decide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. One name** | Parent name is the brewery, restaurant and beer brand ("Parent Brewing & Kitchen") | Simple, scalable, easiest to trademark and market | Less room for sub-identities; name carries everything | Attorney ranking; customer comprehension tests |
| **B. House + sub-brands** | Parent name for the company; restaurant/venue name; separate beer line name | Flexibility for expansion, packaged beer, collaborations | More trademarks, more cost, diluted recognition early | Expansion intent (F-1); packaging path (G-11) |
| **C. Descriptor-led** | A short brand + the descriptor "Indo Mex" as a tagline/what-it-is line | Instantly explains the concept | Descriptor may limit expansion and invites cliché; trademark weakness of descriptive terms | Interview comprehension; attorney view |
**Leaning (provisional, reversible): A or C**, with B reserved for a later packaged-beer line. Not decided.
Name candidates remain in `PARENT-BRAND-CANDIDATES.md` (50) and the screened five (`TRADEMARK-SCREENING.md`); none is promoted.

## 5. Restaurant/venue naming system **[Proposed]**
Space names are descriptive and stable: **Tank Cloister** (brewery hall), **Shade Court**, **Adventure Court** (later), **Game Hall** (later), **the Outlet** (shop). Menu sections named plainly (Small, Tacos, Birria, Quesadillas, Large, Dessert). The place name never contains a culture word as costume. Working names inside the repo (Tank Court etc.) are internal.

## 6. Beer naming philosophy (public-facing detail in `beer/BEER-BRAND-EXPERIENCE-V1.md`)
Public names lead with the **plain style name** (Hefeweizen, Mexican-Style Lager, Vienna Amber, Pilsner, Witbier, Dunkelweizen). Working names (SONA, HAWA, ANGARA) are internal until trademarked. No culture puns; no gods, symbols or costume names. Regional beers named for the idea or ingredient job, verified with advisors.

## 7. Menu naming philosophy (detail in `food/MENU-STORYTELLING-V1.md`)
Keep real dish names (Chicken 65, birria, dal); describe method and inspiration; **label our own compositions as ours**; never use a regional name (Hyderabadi, Malabar, mole, salan) unless the dish meets that tradition's identity test or says "-style / inspired by"; ban "authentic", "exotic", "ancient", "secret".

## 8. Merchandise potential **[Proposed; revenue unproven]**
Collar glass (the signature object; staged manufacturing path in `beer/GLASSWARE-V2.md`), apron/uniform pieces, flight boards, bottle openers, house sauces (DSHS licence question, ledger V-20), prints and posters from commissioned artists (art program `interior/ART-AND-CULTURAL-PROGRAM.md`), cookbooks later. Merch carries the ring/collar and plain wordmark, never cultural icons. **Merch revenue is not evidenced; the BA/Arryved data in the repo show merch per taproom declining (T2/T3).**

## 9. Signage philosophy **[Proposed]**
Quiet and architectural: blank blackened-steel panel until a name exists; later one plain wordmark in steel, lit softly; wayfinding in the grotesque sans; menu and pairing cards on black stoneware-toned stock; **no neon, no string-light signage, no pseudo-script type**; accessibility and Austin sign-code review `[PRO]`.
