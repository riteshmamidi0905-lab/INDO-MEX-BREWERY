# NASHA: Brand Strategy

**Status:** `[PROPOSED]` brand strategy for a **founder-selected** name. **Name status: `SELECTED_BY_FOUNDER — LEGAL CLEARANCE PENDING` (D-62).** Selection is **not** trademark clearance: no trademark is owned, filed or registered; no availability or exclusivity is claimed; no domain or social handle is owned (`ip/founder-asset-register.csv`). If trademark or advisor review finds a **material obstacle, naming returns to the founder; no name is substituted silently.** NASHA is not an operating brewery or restaurant; nothing is built, brewed, cooked, sold or open. Earlier work under the working name "INDO MEX BREWERY" is preserved as history; current-facing documents carry a naming note. Companion: `NASHA-IDENTITY-SYSTEM.md`; the name-meaning and responsibility analysis is §5 below.

## 1. Brand essence, positioning, promise
| Element | Proposed |

## 2. Personality as behaviours (not adjectives)
| Trait | What NASHA *does* | What NASHA does *not* do |
|---|---|---|
| **Curious** | Explains the why of a pairing in one line; publishes experiments and failures | Lecture, hide behind jargon |
| **Exacting** | Names the dish, the heat level, the source; states what is tested and what is not | Exaggerate, say "authentic" |
| **Warm** | Greets, shares, makes families and first-timers comfortable; plain language | Perform hospitality with slogans |
| **Generous** | Credits sources and advisors; shade, shared tables, small plates for sharing | Gatekeep culture or craft |
| **Grounded in Austin** | Uses real materials, weather, light; the place is the setting | Wear Austin (or India, or Mexico) as a costume |
| **Restrained** | One strong colour, a few rules, few words | Shout, pun, decorate |
| **Responsible** | Treats alcohol with care (§5.3) | Glamorise excess |

## 3. Master-brand architecture: three models compared
Criteria are judged by us (not evidence). **+** favourable · **~** mixed · **−** unfavourable.
| Criterion | A. Master brand (NASHA + descriptive language) | B. House + extensions (NASHA Kitchen / Brewing / Labs / Mobile…) | C. Descriptor model (NASHA: Indo-Mex Brewery & Kitchen as the fixed name) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memorability | **+** one word does the work | ~ many names compete | ~ long; the word is diluted |
| Scalability / expansion | **+** descriptors can change, the mark stays | + (if all registered) but costly | − descriptor locks the concept ("brewery" before one exists) |
| Trademark workload | **+** one core mark + descriptive text | − several marks and searches | ~ descriptive text is weak/unprotectable; core mark still needed |
| Food-truck transition | + ("NASHA Indo-Mex Kitchen") | + but a separate "Mobile" brand is unnecessary | − "Brewery & Kitchen" is untrue on a truck |
| Flagship transition | **+** descriptor upgrades when true | ~ | + (but only once true) |
| Merchandise | **+** one mark | ~ | ~ |
| Beer | + (beer line is *by* NASHA; plain style names) | ~ "NASHA Brewing" implies brewing now | ~ |
| Digital identity | **+** one domain/handle family | − fragmented handles | ~ |
| Honesty before a brewery exists | **+** | − "Brewing" before brewing | − |
**Recommendation (provisional, reversible, pending trademark/advisors): Model A.** **NASHA is the brand; a descriptor line changes with the truth of the moment.** Descriptor ladder: *Stage A (now)* "NASHA, an Indo-Mex kitchen and brewery in development" · *Stages B-C* "NASHA Indo-Mex Kitchen" / "NASHA Pop-Up" (event names, not new brands) · *Stage F (only when true)* "NASHA: Indo-Mex Brewery & Kitchen". **Labs** is a **section name**, not a registered sub-brand. **Do not create sub-brands** (NASHA Brewing, NASHA Mobile, NASHA Food Truck) until a real need and trademark coverage exist; "NASHA Brewing" should not be used before brewing exists. Mixed use of `NASHA Brewery & Kitchen` as the *flagship* descriptor remains open.

## 5. The name: meaning, responsibility, and guardrails
### 5.1 Facts and limits
*Nasha* (नशा / نشہ) is a Hindi-Urdu word for **intoxication, with a sense of addiction** in some phrases (dictionary summary, tier T2, `research/VERIFICATION-LEDGER.md` V-33). Nothing in the name signals Mexico. We **do not conceal** this and **do not invent** other linguistic meanings.
### 5.2 Intended interpretation (proposed; for cultural-advisor review)
The brand leans on the *mood* sense, **being captivated**: by flavour, atmosphere, energy, discovery, culture and craft. The meaning is communicated by behaviour (curiosity, craft, hospitality), not by wordplay.
### 5.3 Responsibility commitments (non-negotiable)
NASHA will **never romanticise** addiction, alcoholism, dangerous drinking or substance dependency. In practice: no intoxication or "get drunk/high" language or jokes; no "addicted to NASHA" copy; no shot-drinking or speed-drinking promotions; non-alcoholic options as first-class (aguas, NA beer); 21+ clarity for alcohol; flights and 4-oz pours as a moderation-friendly format; food-first service; rideshare and designated-driver messaging at a real venue; staff trained in responsible service (TABC-certified training, `ATTORNEY-QUESTIONS.md`); no cannabis-style or nightlife branding. The brand voice never uses the word *nasha* for alcohol effects.
### 5.4 Risk register for the name
| Risk | Mitigation | Tripwire (returns to founder) |
|---|---|---|
| Reads as normalising addiction | Responsibility commitments; advisor review; copy rules; a clear "about the name" page | Advisors or attorney judge it a material obstacle |
| Reads Indian-only; Mexico invisible | Descriptor "Indo-Mex"; visual language carries both; test comprehension in interviews | Interviews show most guests read it as an Indian-only brand |
| Alcohol-advertising scrutiny | Attorney review of beer-related copy | Regulator or attorney objection |
| Trademark conflicts | IP attorney knockout; no use before clearance | Material conflict found |
| Cultural misreading (Hindi/Urdu connotation in a family venue) | Advisors; avoid the family-venue conflict by messaging | Advisor recommendation to change |
### 5.5 Brand guardrails: what NASHA must never become
| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| A stereotypical Indian restaurant or Mexican cantina | Costume, not craft |
| A generic industrial brewery | No distinctiveness |
| Nightclub or cannabis-style branding | Conflicts with the name's responsibility stance and family positioning |
| Addiction-glorifying branding | Core ethical rule |
| A gimmicky fusion restaurant | The pairing logic must earn its place |
| So serious and luxurious that it loses warmth | The promise is "stay a while" |
| Halal-signalling by implication (no halal claim) | D-54 |

## 6. Voice (with examples; all proposed)
Principles: plain, specific, present tense, honest about status; explain the why in one line; credit sources; never "authentic/exotic/ancient/secret"; no puns about either culture; no intoxication jokes.
| Context | Example |
|---|---|
| Homepage | "NASHA is being built. Food and beer, designed together, for Austin." |
| Menu | "**Tandoori Lamb Taco.** Our own dish: charred, yogurt-marinated lamb in a flour tortilla. In development." |
| Beer | "**Hefeweizen.** Designed to show banana and clove in balance. Not yet brewed." |
| Social | "Test 1 of the paneer taco: the marinade held, the tortilla didn't. Notes from the kitchen." *(only after it happens)* |
| Signage | "NASHA. Indo-Mex kitchen." / "Water is free." |
| Investor communication | "What is real, what is assumed, what is unvalidated, on every page." |

## 7. Cultural philosophy
India × Mexico must feel **integrated, not decorative**: through ingredients, technique and craft, never costume. Austin is the setting: shade, limestone, live oaks, long tables. Where cultures are compared, we say it is a **cooking-logic analogy**, not a shared history; where we create something new we say it is **ours** (`CULTURAL-INTEGRITY-AND-ADVISORS.md`). Advisors review before anything is public.

## 8. Naming-transition rules
Historical documents (phase-one, decisions, research, logs, earlier visuals) keep "INDO MEX BREWERY" as provenance. Current/future-facing documents show NASHA, with the legacy name noted once. No global search-and-replace. Public materials never imply an operating brewery before one exists.
