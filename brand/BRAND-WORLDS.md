# Three Brand Worlds and a Recommendation

Why this exists: the Phase One look (terracotta, brass, geometric tile, agave, lattice) is attractive but generic "upscale fusion". These worlds start from **ownable ideas**, not decorative objects. All `[CONCEPT]`.

## Anchor facts that any world can use (research)
- Chile reached India from the Americas in the 16th century; mango and tamarind reached Mexico via Asian trade; marigold is native to Mexico and central to Indian ritual and decoration. Details and caveats in `research/PAIRING-MATRIX-V2.md`.
- Wheat flatbreads meet corn masa; Punjabi-Mexican families have lived in California since 1916; al pastor shows Mexico absorbing a migrant technique. `[V S-36,S-37,S-48]`
- Both cultures have a **black-clay pottery tradition** (Oaxaca's barro negro; Indian black pottery such as Nizamabad in Telangana and Longpi in Manipur) `[U: verify]`, and both lay **terrazzo/granito floors** `[U]`. These are shared material truths, not symbols.
- The Tropic of Cancer crosses both countries `[V partial]`.
- Austin context: ~29-33 days a year at 100°F+, and 69 such days in summer 2023 `[V S-40]`; brewery openings slowing `[V S-27a]`.

## WORLD A: FIRE & FERMENT
- **Idea:** the brewery as a working fire-and-steel machine. Tank hall, tandoor and comal glowing.
- **Architecture:** raw, industrial; blackened steel, concrete, big glass; tanks forward; fire visible.
- **Beer packaging:** matte black cans with a single bold ember-orange band; massive condensed name; minimal.
- **Food presentation:** char, steam, dark stoneware, hands-on.
- **Glassware:** heavy-based mug and the hero vase with a thick base ring.
- **Logo direction:** stencil/industrial wordmark with a ring-in-square mark.
- **Materials:** blackened steel, board-formed concrete, charred cedar, copper kettles.
- **Lighting:** low amber, flame, tanks uplit.
- **Music:** blues, rock, indie, DJs.
- **Uniforms:** black canvas aprons, rolled sleeves.
- **Merch:** workwear, enamel mugs.
- **Day:** dim, moody (risk: too dark for families and for heat management).
- **Night:** spectacular.
- **Perception:** young, adult, brewery-geek, male-skewing risk.
- **National scalability:** high (industrial aesthetic travels), but looks like every industrial brewery without the cultural layer.

## WORLD B: COURT & MONSOON
- **Idea:** the courtyard as climate machine: shade, air, water, plants.
- **Architecture:** low, deep arcades, rill water, trees, covered edges; tanks seen through glass.
- **Packaging:** softer palette, rain and wheat patterns, light cans.
- **Food:** shared tables, wide bowls.
- **Glassware:** tall hefe vase; delicate.
- **Logo:** courtyard-plan mark.
- **Materials:** limestone, cedar, concrete, water.
- **Lighting:** dappled day, lantern night.
- **Music:** acoustic, jazz, world, family-friendly.
- **Uniforms:** linen, indigo aprons.
- **Merch:** totes, linen.
- **Day:** best of all worlds.
- **Night:** beautiful but similar to many string-light patios unless the architecture is distinctive.
- **Perception:** relaxed, broad, families, couples.
- **Scalability:** medium; strongly site-dependent (not every location has space/climate).

## WORLD C: GRAIN & SUN
- **Idea:** ingredient-first: wheat, corn, malt, chile, sun.
- **Architecture:** bright, contemporary, daylight-flooded hall; farmhouse-modern.
- **Packaging:** bright, color-blocked cans by grain/crop; clear icons.
- **Food:** ingredient-forward, colorful.
- **Glassware:** clean, minimal.
- **Logo:** simple geometric sun-grain mark.
- **Materials:** pale wood, plaster, stone, terrazzo.
- **Lighting:** daylight.
- **Music:** indie, acoustic, brunch DJs.
- **Uniforms:** natural linen/denim.
- **Merch:** sunny graphics.
- **Day:** excellent.
- **Night:** weakest; the tanks lose drama.
- **Perception:** modern American craft; the least culturally specific.
- **Scalability:** very high, but least ownable.

## Recommendation: controlled hybrid, "TANK COURT" ("A's spine, B's climate, C's clarity")
1. **From A:** tanks and fire are the icon. The Tank Hall is the spine of the building and of every photograph.
2. **From B:** the courtyard principle governs the outdoor experience, designed as a *climate device*.
3. **From C:** a clear, bright, ingredient-honest voice in packaging and menus (grain, corn, chile named plainly) so the brand reads as modern American craft at national scale.
4. **The ownable signatures** (details in `visuals/VISUAL-DNA.md`): **the Tank Cloister** (fermenters as an arcade), **the marigold band** (one saturated color), **black stoneware** (shared pottery tradition), **terrazzo with volcanic aggregate** floor, **the ring-collar glass**, **ring-in-square** graphic.

Why not choose one world: A alone is a generic industrial brewery; B alone is a pleasant patio; C alone is bright but anonymous. Together, with the marigold + black + stainless palette, they become recognizable in a single photograph.

## What we deliberately dropped from Phase One
Terracotta walls, brass accents everywhere, geometric Moorish-style tile, agave plants as props, generic jaali lattice as decoration. These can reappear only if they serve a function (e.g., shade screens that really shade).
