# Courtyard wordmark vs glass-inspired variation (EXPLORATION — neither is final)

Idea: both A's keep the arch + crossbar (still an A) but the arch top is cut free from the legs by a thin horizontal gap — the head of foam on a poured beer. No glass is drawn.
G1 = small head, G2 = taller head. `comparison-true-size.png` shows original / G1 / G2 at cap heights 16, 24, 32, 96, 256 px (true pixels) plus a 4× zoom.
Generated by `brand/tools/logo_glass_a.py`, `glyph_readability.py` (metrics → `readability.json`), `glass_comparison_board.py`.

## Findings
- OCR cannot read ANY of the custom wordmarks (original included), so OCR is not a usable test. The first correlation test was invalid and is marked superseded in code.
- Metrics used: foam-gap thickness in px and silhouette overlap (IoU) with the original A. Gap ≥ 1.28 px at 16 px (small cut), IoU 0.88–0.95. This is a shape-damage proxy, not a user test.
- By eye: NASHA stays readable in all variants at all five sizes. The glass versions look busier at ≤ 24 px (the gap turns into grey noise) and at ≥ 96 px the cap can read as a hat/accent on the A.
- **Recommendation (for your review):** keep the original as the primary wordmark. G1 is a viable optional "beer-context" alternate for large formats (≥ 96 px cap height: can fronts, glass etching, signage) but not for small digital, menus or any use ≤ 32 px. G2's taller head drifts further from a clean A.
