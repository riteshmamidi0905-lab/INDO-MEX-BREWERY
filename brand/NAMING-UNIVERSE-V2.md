# Beer Naming Universe v2 (119 candidates, supersedes the Phase One list; old file kept)

**Status: every name is UNCLEARED.** "Prelim TM risk" is a rough guess from how common the word is (High = crowded word), **not** a search result. No USPTO, TTB COLA, Texas SOS or domain searches were run. `[PRO]` trademark attorney search required before adoption.
Meanings come from general knowledge of the languages `[U]`; a native-speaker review (Hindi/Urdu, Mexican Spanish, Nahuatl) is required. Avoided on purpose: names of deities (Agni, Surya, Pavan, Ganga etc.), sacred rivers, living communities' names (e.g., Sardar), and obvious existing beer/brand names.
Pronunciation = approximate English.

## Criteria used
Pronounceable by Americans · ≤3 syllables where possible (tap handles) · strong on a can · not stereotype-dependent · not obviously religious · not generic "fusion" · not childish.

## Re-evaluating BADSHAH and RAJA: what is the *energy*?

**Why they feel powerful `[C]`:** (1) two syllables, hard stress on the first, with a plosive opener (B- / R-J) and a long open final vowel: they land like a drumbeat; (2) the meaning is instantly legible to Americans (king, emperor) and thus requires no explanation; (3) *BAD*-shah borrows English "bad" as in "bad-ass", giving swagger; (4) both are ceremony words: someone *arrives*.
**Qualities to preserve:** confidence, ceremony, pride, scale, memorability; short, percussive, easy to shout across a bar.

**Practical problems `[PRO]`:** BADSHAH is the stage name of a living Indian musician and, we believe, appears in existing spice/consumer branding (verify). RAJA is extremely common (given name, many businesses). Royalty also invites the "maharaja palace" cliché the founder wants to avoid.

**Recommendation:** Do not make royalty the brand's spine. Keep BADSHAH as a *possible* limited-release name only if a trademark and cultural review clears it; otherwise pursue the same energy through **ceremony and celebration**:
- **DHOL** (Punjabi wedding drum) and **BANDA** (Mexican brass-band) are the two most powerful *cross-cultural* finds: loud, communal, ceremonial instruments of celebration, one from each culture. Pairing `DHOL × BANDA` is a strong *system* concept (e.g., Hefeweizen = DHOL? Mexican lager = BANDA?). `[C]` Needs cultural review; "Dhol" is neutral, "Banda" has other slang meanings to check.
- **BARAAT** (wedding procession), **JALWA** (splendor), **BRÍO** (spirit), **TOOFAN** (storm), **ANGARA** (ember), **SAMRAT** (emperor, less used than Raja).
- **Invented power words** can also work (e.g., SOLVARA) but have no inherited meaning.
