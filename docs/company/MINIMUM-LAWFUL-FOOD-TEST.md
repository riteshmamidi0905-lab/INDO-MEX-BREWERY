# Minimum real-world requirements for the first customer-facing food test (orientation, NOT legal advice)
Prepared by a regulatory-research AI agent, 2026-10-11. Every rule below is T1 (web-search summaries; Austin Public Health, Texas DSHS and Austin Fire pages could not be opened from this sandbox) and unverified. Nothing concludes that anything is permitted, licensable or compliant; the authorities and an attorney decide. Alcohol is excluded from all tiers until the Texas alcohol attorney answers ATT-6 and Q-05. Use the neutral project label, not the NASHA name (D-78), and no logo (D-66). On any document seen by a taster, supplier or authority, describe the activity as "a private recipe-development test (concept stage; no restaurant or brewery exists)". The working label is an internal file identifier only. Menu: Core 5 food only; no beef or pork; no halal claim; no mustard oil. The first step is internal cooking (Sessions 1-5), which is a recipe test, not a customer event. See the regulatory tree: `docs/company/REGULATORY-QUESTION-TREE.md`.

Reported sources (T1, accessed 2026-10-11, search summaries only): Austin Public Health temporary food events and mobile vendor pages (a permit is reportedly generally needed if an event is open to the public, publicly advertised including ticket sales or social media, or serves open food; organiser applies at least 10 calendar days ahead; older guidance says no home-prepared foods; fees and commissary rules not read); the reported Texas DSHS takeover of mobile-unit permits on 2026-07-01 (local permits issued earlier reportedly not valid after that date); Austin Fire pages (a Temporary Change of Use to Public Assembly reportedly needed for 50 or more people in a building without a matching certificate of occupancy; reported lead times of 21 and 30 days conflict; propane or grease-producing units reportedly need a fire inspection); Texas cottage-food summary (meat, poultry, dairy and other temperature-controlled foods reportedly excluded; DSHS must confirm).

**Questions for Austin Public Health or an attorney:** (1) does a free, invite-only tasting at home need anything; (2) does a cost-share or ticket make it a sale; (3) may a commissary be used for tastings and what must be on file; (4) does the 2026-07-01 DSHS transition change the route; (5) does fire apply below 50 people; (6) what liability exposure does the host carry.
