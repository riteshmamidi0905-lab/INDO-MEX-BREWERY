### Do both Mexican-Style Lager and Pilsner deserve taps? (challenged, neither removed)
- **For both.** Different jobs: chile relief (low bitterness) vs fried-food reset (dry, hop aroma). Pilsner is the credibility beer; Mexican Lager is the volume beer.
- **Against.** Both are pale, clean, brilliant lagers on the same glass family and the same long cold-tank cycle. The Mexican Lager IBU window (14-18) is below BJCP 2A (18-25) and BA (15-22) and looks chosen to widen the gap (00-BREWER-HANDOFF.md section 0c); if a brewer raises it, the gap narrows.
- **Guest view.** A guest may see "two light lagers". The difference must show on the tap board and in the glass; the pairing use (PF-01 vs PF-07) is the proof of purpose.
- **Cheapest honest test.** Triangle test in 00-BREWER-HANDOFF.md section 0d (design only, NOT RUN) plus the PF-01/PF-07 use question. Needs a lawfully served stand-in (attorney).
- **Rule (proposal).** Keep both until tested. On failure: rework once, retest; then consider one lager plus a hop variant (needs brewer and sales data) or a clearly different seasonal in the second slot. Removal is a founder decision with the brewer's view.

### Portfolio read
- Observed: two wheat ales, three lagers; lager share drives tank occupancy (opening program section 6).
- Inferred: five dedicated fermenters may not be needed; sharing is an open brewer question.
- Unknown: whether any partner or pilot slot can run lagers. This is not a volume forecast.

**Requirements so the NA guest is equally considered (design rules).**
1. Equal glassware tier to the beer pour (own variant of the family; no logo or mark on glassware, D-66). Not a pint glass of ice.
2. Equal menu placement: in the same pairing line per dish, same typography and row. No separate "other drinks" list.
3. Equal explanation: what it is, what it does with the dish (one line), allergens.
4. Same small-pour logic as the beer; price logic equal in spirit (no price set here).
5. Staff describe each NA pour as well as each beer (training is Lane A).
6. No soda afterthought; a table of four with one non-drinker gets a real pairing.
7. No imitation-beer framing, no health claims, no kitsch "mocktail" wording (founder wording decision).

**Unknowns (verify with a Texas alcohol attorney; nothing concluded here).**

- Whether a licensed third-party NA beer is sourced or sold under different rules.
- Whether house hop water or any fermented drink triggers alcohol or food-code rules.
- Allergen, juice and produce handling for house aguas (food-safety professional).
- Costs, shelf life, supply: unknown.

## 3. "Two-Pour" reassessment

**Repo facts.** "Two-Pour" already names the Hefeweizen pour technique and is also the proposed pairing idea. The brand review flags that it normalises two drinks per dish, risks "always two", and that nasha can read as intoxication, so a ritual frame is risky. Current default: one small pour, a second optional, an equal NA pour.

**RECOMMENDATION (reversible; not a public rename; founder approves any naming).**
- "Two-Pour" is not the right guest-facing idea. Keep it as an internal working concept at most.
- Default guest experience: **one small pairing pour per dish (A/E)**, with the beer and the NA pour listed together on the same dish line. "Pairing pour" is a plain placeholder label, not an approved name.
- **B on request** (a second, different small pour), with server prompts about water, food and pace; no automatic upsell.
- **C as a learning test** at one dish (beer vs NA side by side).
- **D (flight)** is a separate product if wanted; do not merge it with the pairing idea.
- Reserve "Two-Pour" for the Hefeweizen pour technique, or retire it if the overlap confuses.
- Verify with a Texas alcohol attorney and for server training before any wording about quantity or pairing rounds.
- Would change if: guest evidence appears (none exists), staff find the default hard to run, or POS limits bite.
