# Brand experience system and responsible-alcohol design (PROPOSED)
Prepared by a brand-director AI agent from the repository. Nothing here has been built, tasted, tested or professionally reviewed. No logo, mark or lockup is implied (D-66); any NASHA wordmark is a temporary typographic placeholder. The name is founder-selected with legal clearance pending. "Must avoid" and "standard" cells are design hypotheses to test, not facts. Legal and insurance questions are flagged "verify with a Texas alcohol attorney / insurance broker"; none is answered here.

## 1. Brand experience system
**Target feel:** warm, curious, social, craft-driven, confident, Austin, culturally respectful. **Anti-feel:** theme park, nightclub, luxury theatre, generic brewery, Indian costume, Mexican cantina stereotype. **Rule of thumb:** show the work (real brewing, real kitchen); never perform a culture. Cultural storytelling uses only verified facts and named cooks and makers, and cultural advisors own it. No scripted gimmicks: no required greeting, no mandatory story, no forced tableside show.
Pop-up column: **Y** testable at a pop-up, **P** partly, **N** needs a built venue.

| Touchpoint | Should feel | Concrete standard (to test) | Must avoid | Pop-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arrival | Easy, shaded, obvious | Shade or a clear covered approach; wayfinding by light; no queue in the sun | hype lines, fog or neon, costume doormen | P |
| Host greeting | Welcomed, not processed | Greeting within seconds of eye contact (a target to measure, not a promise); honest wait time and seat choice | memorised speeches, exoticising language | Y |
| Menu | Clear in about a minute | One page, plain beer styles, heat scale 0-3, dietary and allergen marks, "no beef or pork" stated, no halal claim; goat reads as goat | decorative "authentic" claims, unverified history, bazaar or cantina clichés | Y |
| Ordering | Low friction | One obvious way to order per zone; staff can explain every item | ordering confusion, upsell scripts | Y |
| Beer explanation | Approachable for novices, rich for enthusiasts | Three lines per beer: tastes like, goes with, strength shown; a brewer is named only if the brewer exists | jargon walls, effect or strength as a selling point | Y |
| Food arrival | Paced, shareable | Shareables first; dishes named only if a guest looks unsure | everything at once, theatre for its own sake | Y |
| Pairing explanation | Curious, not preachy | One plain sentence per pairing; "we suggest", never "you must" | claims of perfect matches before tasting | P |
| Tableware | Honest, tactile, durable | Plain glass and plates; the correct glass family once decided (Glass V2 is a concept) | costume ceramics, "ethnic" prints, brand marks on ware | P |
| Music | Present but conversational | A table of four can talk without raising voices (check by observation) | club volume, sitar or cantina playlists | Y |
| Lighting | Warm evening glow | Warm white, low glare, food and faces readable | neon, colour-wash theatrics, dark-bar mood | P |
| Service | Knowledgeable, unhurried, equal to everyone | Staff answer allergen and no-beef/pork questions straight or say "let me check"; same warmth for families, regulars, tourists | scripted banter, performed accents, pushing rounds | Y |
| Patio | Shared, shaded, relaxed | Communal tables, shade, fans; family and bar areas legible | party-patio feel, unsupervised play near the bar | P |
| Check / payment | Quiet and fair | Easy split checks; water and NA items clear; tip policy is a founder decision | "one more" prompts, surprise fees | Y |
| Departure | Settled, safe | Warm farewell without a script; ride options visible | pushing a last drink | P |
Pop-up measures to reuse: time to first beer, wait quality, can guests say what the place is, shareable choice, a three-question exit card. Not testable at a pop-up: brewery visibility, drive reveal and other built-venue features.

## 2. Responsible-alcohol design
**Lesson (Sprint 18, concept image A5):** a render showed children at a table with beer pints. It was accepted for internal reference only, with the responsible-alcohol and public-use concern noted. Rule: no image may show a minor and alcohol together as a joint subject.
- **Family seating:** seat by party type with clear sightlines; bar seating adult-focused; beer is never placed in front of a minor in any layout, photo or menu graphic. Evening policy for family areas is a proposal: verify minors rules with a Texas alcohol attorney / TABC specialist.
- **Beer imagery and marketing photography checklist:** no minors with alcohol; nobody drinking at speed or in volume; no pairing drinking with driving, sport or work; no implied effect (relaxed, buzzed, euphoric); a food or NA drink visible in most shots; adult models clearly of age; show process (tanks, glass, pour, table) not volume; AI renders stay concept-only. Verify advertising and label rules with a Texas alcohol attorney.
- **NA availability and equal standing:** at least one NA beer or equivalent, a house soft drink and water on the same page in the same type size, with the same pairing line as the beers; offered with the same warmth. How many NA items is a founder decision. Verify NA brewing and labelling position with an attorney.
- **Pairing pours and flights:** small, defined pours with volumes listed (TBD with the brewer and attorney); flights are for tasting, not quantity; water by default, food before or with; never framed as a challenge or "finish the set". Verify serving size, flight practice and liability exposure with an attorney and insurance broker.
- **Server language, never:** "one more", "another round?" as a push, "hooked", "addicted", "crave", "intoxicating", "buzz", "get you going", "loosen up", any promise of effect, strength as a benefit. Say instead: "would you like to see the menu again?", "can I bring water?", "what would go with your plate?". Staff may decline service and are backed by management. Training must be owned by a qualified trainer (verify required certification with an attorney). Test by role-playing a pop-up service and checking the script against the banned list.
- **Minors:** welcome only where law and policy allow (policy unresolved); no minor handles, pours or is offered alcohol; soft drinks for children are not styled like beer. Verify with an attorney and an insurance broker (liquor liability, play areas).
- **Events:** same NA, water and food rules; no drinking games, unlimited-drink formats or volume contests; safe-departure options shown at close; host responsibilities in writing for private events. Verify with an attorney / insurance broker.
- **The name:** NASHA can mean intoxication or addiction. Copy defines it through the essence (food and beer designed together, plain sight, a long table), never through effect or compulsion. Banned in copy: "get NASHA", "addicted to NASHA", "feel the NASHA", "NASHA hits", puns on dependence. Make no claim of clearance or exclusivity. Verify with a trademark attorney and cultural advisors.
**Pre-launch checks (all untested):** 100% of marketing images pass the checklist; NA items match beer items in size and position; zero banned words in menu, website, social and staff scripts; a role-play where a server declines service and offers water without friction; the open items go to the attorney and broker (minors, pour sizes, advertising, NA labelling, events, dram shop, liquor liability, play-area insurance).
