## 1. Premise
Indian-Mexican food already exists in Austin: a restaurant named Nasha, Sivestar Indian Tacos, Kurry Takos, Taco Twist, Austin Taco Project, Urban Turban (Round Rock), and Whip In as Indian-influenced food with beer (all T1, status unconfirmed; `data/market-map.csv` rows 16-21). **NASHA must not claim to have invented, pioneered or been first in Indian-Mexican fusion.** The only open question the repo data supports (H1, untested) is the combination of an in-house brewery with a kitchen that has a stated Indian-Mexican logic, and that rests on a capped search. Chef-led kitchens at breweries also exist (The Brewer's Table, Fitzhugh; T1).

## 3. Ranking (agent judgement, not data)
**Three most defensible, if proven**
1. **Food and beer co-development.** The combination is the one thing not found in the Austin data (H1) and needs a brewer, a chef and time together. Caveat: The Brewer's Table is a pairing-oriented precedent (T1, 2018-era, status unknown), so the claim must be about NASHA's specific Core 5 system, not the idea.
2. **Culinary execution quality.** Lives in skill and consistency (e.g. Goat Birria that reads as goat, a Chicken 65 chip that stays crisp). Needs V01, V02, V03 results first.
3. **Non-alcoholic pairing parity.** A design stance needing training and real recipes. No Austin venue doing this was found, but that is absence of data, not proof.

**Three weakest claims**
1. **Beer-style differentiation.** Styles are common (Live Oak hefeweizen, Celis witbier; T1/LOW), anyone can brew them, and the repo questions whether Pilsner and Mexican-Style Lager are distinguishable.
2. **Core 5 product discipline.** A short menu is a choice any operator can make, and the Core 5 may be cut to 3-4 items anyway.
3. **Guest education.** Cards and server lines are trivially copied and do not alone bring a guest in. (Service experience is nearly as weak for the same reason.)

| Also avoid: "award-winning", "best", "tested", "chef-approved", "brewed", "halal", "traditional" for any NASHA item; any claim that pairing "works"; any exclusivity claim for the name. | Nothing has occurred; D-54 forbids a halal claim; the name is not cleared. |

Neutral rule: describe NASHA by what it intends to do and what has been tested, never by what others lack.
