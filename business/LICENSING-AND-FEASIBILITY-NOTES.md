# Licensing Implications and Feasibility Findings (NOT legal advice)

Assumption (D-08): **destination brewpub first**; keep canning, packaged beer, retail distribution, additional sites and interstate growth possible. Every legal point below needs a Texas alcohol attorney and TABC confirmation `[PRO]`. Documents retrieved are 2013-2021 vintage; some sources were summaries.

## 1. What the sources say about the Texas brewpub path
| Topic | Finding | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| License structure | Brewpub License (BP) is **subordinate** to a primary: Wine & Malt Beverage Retailer's Permit (BG), Mixed Beverage Permit (MB) or Retail Dealer's On-Premise License (BE) | S-01, S-02 | Medium-High (TABC training docs, 2020-21) |
| Activities | May brew, bottle, can, package and label malt beverages; serve own beer on premises; operate a restaurant; take own beer to tastings/competitions | S-01 | Medium (summary) |
| Production cap | **10,000 barrels** per brewpub per year per TABC description; raised from 5,000 by SB 515 (83R) | S-01, S-04 | Medium; verify in current statute |
| Off-premise sales | Reported: containers up to ½ barrel | S-01 summary | Low-Medium |
| Sales to wholesalers/retailers | §74.08 allows a brewpub meeting its conditions (BG holder; own-premises malt beverages) to sell its beer to wholesalers/distributors/retailers; SB 515 versions discussed caps (e.g., 1,000 bbl self-distribution) **not confirmed as enacted** | S-38, S-04 | Low-Medium. **Corrects Phase One statement** |
| Wine | 2017 HB 2097 amended §74.08 so brewpubs can sell wine produced by others | S-38b | Medium |
| Guest beer | Not authorized by §74.08 as read; a BG permit holder generally sells beer/wine, but interplay with the brewpub conditions is unclear | S-38 | **Unresolved `[PRO]`** |
| Alternative path | Brewer's Permit (manufacturer) sells to wholesalers; a retail tier needs separate structure | S-03, S-05 | Medium |

## 2. Design implications that keep the future open (no legal conclusions)
- Size the cellar, floor drains, glycol and CO₂ for a possible **canning line** location, even if installed later.
- Keep production on a separately identifiable entity/location plan so a later Brewer's Permit or packaging facility is possible `[PRO: attorney]`.
- Choose a brand that is not building-specific (parent brand ≠ architectural idea).
- Reserve loading, cold storage and truck access for kegs/cases (OPERATIONAL-FLOW diagram).
- Texas three-tier law and distributor franchise rules affect interstate growth `[PRO]`.

## 3. Feasibility findings from research
1. **Market consolidation:** Texas breweries fell 440 to 420 (2024-25); openings under 25/yr vs 50 in 2023; at least 25 closures in each of 2024-25; causes include capital cost, interest rates, real estate and ingredients, cautious lenders. North Austin brewery count fell 7 to 3 in about a year. `[V S-27a,b]`
2. **Heat:** 69 days ≥100°F in summer 2023 and a 45-day streak at Camp Mabry; 30-year average ~33 days/yr. Outdoor design must be climate-engineered. `[V S-40]`
3. **Cautionary example from India:** Bira 91's growth on contract production and heavy spend ended in going-concern doubt, unpaid staff and a partner exit (2024-25 reporting). `[V S-22]`
4. **Competition:** Pinthouse is Austin's largest brewery by volume (23,000 bbl in 2024), Lazarus proves taco + brewpub demand; neither offers a cuisine-led, architecture-led destination. `[V S-30, S-29]`
5. **Capex risk:** visible brewhouse + full kitchen + large court is a premium build. Costs unresearched `[Unresolved: Phase Four; architect/contractor/brewhouse vendor quotes]`.
6. **Glassware** custom molds need 10,000+ units: merchandise strategy required `[V S-56]`.
